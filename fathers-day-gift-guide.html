Father's Day Gift Guide for Dads Who Grill

Skip the novelty apron. Real, built-to-last gifts for grill dads, plus a Father's Day cookout menu he will not have to cook himself.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Father's Day is practically a national grilling holiday. For many dads, the grill is a source of pride, a place to relax and a way to feed the people they love. That makes a grilling gift a natural choice. But the market is flooded with gimmicks: novelty aprons, flimsy tool sets and gadgets that break after one use. This guide focuses on gifts that a grill dad will actually use and appreciate.

Think about the dad

Different dads, different gifts:

  • The backyard pitmaster: loves long cooks and big gatherings.
  • The weeknight griller: wants quick, delicious dinners.
  • The adventurer: grills at campsites, beaches and tailgates.
  • The new dad: short on time, needs efficient tools.
  • The tinkerer: loves gear, technique and experimenting.
  • The small-space dad: lives in an apartment or condo.

Gifts that are not things

Many dads say they want time more than stuff. Consider:

  • Cook for him. Take over the grill for the day and make him a feast.
  • A cookout with his friends. Invite his buddies over, handle the logistics, and let him enjoy it.
  • A day off. Clean the grill, restock the charcoal and handle the cleanup.
  • A lesson. Learn a new technique together, like the reverse sear or a spatchcocked chicken.

A Father's Day cookout menu (that dad does not cook)

Appetizer

Grilled shrimp skewers with garlic butter. Thread shrimp on double skewers, brush with garlic butter, grill 2 minutes per side.

Main

Reverse-seared ribeye. Dry brine the night before, cook slowly on the coal-free half of a two-zone fire, then sear over the coals. Slice and serve with chimichurri.

Sides

  • Coal-roasted potatoes in foil with butter and rosemary.
  • Grilled asparagus with lemon and parmesan.
  • Wedge salad with blue cheese dressing, bacon and tomatoes.

Dessert

Grilled peaches with vanilla ice cream and a drizzle of bourbon caramel.

Drinks

His favorite beer, a classic old fashioned, or fresh lemonade.

Kid-made gifts

Younger kids can make meaningful gifts for grill dads:

  • A homemade rub. Help kids mix a simple rub of brown sugar, paprika, salt, pepper, garlic powder and onion powder. Put it in a jar with a hand-drawn label.
  • A recipe book. Collect family favorite recipes and illustrate them.
  • A "grill master" certificate.
  • A decorated apron. Fabric markers on a plain canvas apron.

Why a durable gift matters

Grill dads appreciate tools that last. A thick steel grill built to take heat and heavy pans will be around for years, used at countless birthdays, holidays and Saturday dinners. Some gifts are forgotten in a drawer. A good grill becomes part of the family.

The takeaway

The best Father's Day gift for a grill dad is something useful, well made and personal. Whether it is a new Slot Grill, a griddle upgrade, a precise thermometer or a day off from cooking, give him something that celebrates what he loves.

Going further: turning the gift into a day

Plan a Father's Day cookout he does not have to run

Many dads end up working the grill at their own celebration. Flip the script: give the grill in the morning, then do the cooking yourself, with him supervising from a chair. A simple menu that showcases a new grill:

  • Starter: grilled sausages sliced into coins with mustard.
  • Main: reverse-seared ribeyes, rested under a coin of garlic-herb butter.
  • Side: grilled corn and a big tomato salad.
  • Dessert: grilled peaches with vanilla ice cream.

Build a two-zone fire so the steaks can coast on the cool side while you grill the corn over the coals. Use an instant-read thermometer to pull the steaks at 115°F to 120°F before the sear for medium-rare.

Gifts that create a ritual

The best gifts start traditions:

  • A "first Saturday" cookout. Promise one grill night a month together.
  • A recipe journal. A blank notebook for him to record cooks, temperatures and notes. Within a year it becomes a family heirloom.
  • A season of steak. A monthly visit to the butcher together to pick a new cut.

For the dad who has everything

If he already owns a full kitchen of tools, look for things that solve a specific gap:

  • A griddle plate for an existing Slot Grill, from $20, to try flat-top breakfasts.
  • A second, smaller grill for camping or tailgates, such as the Compact at $79.97.
  • A thermometer that lives on the fridge door, thanks to its magnetic back.

For grandfathers

Grandfathers often appreciate gifts that involve grandkids. Plan a kid-friendly cookout: hot dogs, grilled pineapple and s'mores over the last of the coals. Adults handle the fire and the hot steel; kids handle toppings and taste-testing. Take photos and frame one.

Writing the card

A few words make the gift. Try one of these:

  • "For all the burgers you flipped for us. Now it is our turn."
  • "Here is to many more Saturday nights by the fire."
  • "Your recipe, your grill, our table."
