The Kitchen & Cookout FAQ

Charcoal, carbon steel, temperatures, gifting, and how the Slot Grill fits into a real home kitchen.

A cooking magazine for home cooks and gift buyers. We publish recipes, technique guides, carbon steel care and gift ideas, built around one piece of gear we think deserves a spot in every kitchen: the Slot Grill.

Why do you only feature the Slot Grill?

Because this site is about one find, not a list of fifty. We would rather explain one well-made tool in depth than skim across dozens.

What is a Slot Grill?

A charcoal grill made from thick carbon steel plates that slide together with tabs and slots. It needs no tools, no hinges and no screws, and it sets up in roughly 30 to 60 seconds.

What sizes does it come in?

Two: the Compact, with a 16.25 × 9 in. grate that suits a household of one to three, and the Full Size, with a 20.5 × 11.25 in. grate for families and groups.

What does a Slot Grill sell for?

The Compact starts at $79.97 and the Full Size at $139.97. Versions with one or two griddle plates cost more; see the product pages for current options.

How heavy are they?

The Compact, in 12-gauge steel, is listed at about 14 pounds. The Full Size, in 10-gauge steel, is listed at about 27.6 pounds.

How thin is it once packed?

Roughly an inch deep for the Compact and an inch and a quarter for the Full Size.

Where is it made?

In the USA, in Texas.

Is shipping free?

Grills list free shipping within the lower 48 states. Orders typically leave the shop in 1 to 3 days.

Can I put cast iron on it?

Yes. Both sizes are built heavy enough to hold a cast-iron skillet or Dutch oven over the coals.

Does it have a lid?

No. It is an open charcoal grill. Direct cooking is fast; indirect cooking on the cool side works but is slower than in a covered kettle, so plan extra time for thick cuts.

What is the griddle add-on?

A carbon steel plate that sits on the grill to create a flat-top surface. Use one plate beside the grate or two for a full griddle. It is sold as an add-on; the grill is separate.

Does carbon steel rust?

It can if left wet. Keep it dry, wipe it with a thin coat of oil after cooking and store it somewhere ventilated. Surface rust scrubs off and the steel can be re-seasoned.

What is the Seasoning Balm?

A blend of avocado oil and beeswax made for carbon steel and cast iron cookware. Wipe on the thinnest film you can, give it an hour at 400°F, and do another round. It also serves as a food-safe alternative to barbecue paint on the grill body.

What thermometer do you recommend?

The Slot Grill Instant-Read Thermometer: about 3-second reads, ±1.8°F accuracy, a -58°F to 572°F range, a backlit screen and a magnetic back. Hand wash only and do not submerge it.

What internal temperatures should I cook to?

Our guides follow USDA guidance: 165°F for poultry, 160°F for ground meat, and 145°F for whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb and veal with a 3-minute rest. Fish is done at 145°F.

Can I use the grill indoors?

No. Charcoal produces carbon monoxide. Only grill outdoors in open air, never in a garage, tent or enclosed porch.

Can I use it on a balcony?

Check your lease and local fire codes first. Many buildings restrict charcoal on balconies.

Lump charcoal or briquettes?

Both work. Lump lights faster and burns hotter; briquettes burn more evenly and longer. See our charcoal guide for details.
