Lemon-Herb Grilled Chicken Thighs

Juicy, crisp-skinned bone-in thighs with a bright marinade and a two-stage fire plan that ends flare-ups for good.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

If you only learn one chicken recipe for the grill, make it bone-in, skin-on thighs. They are cheap, almost impossible to dry out, and they reward patience with crackling skin and meat so tender it pulls off the bone. Breasts get the spotlight, but thighs are what experienced backyard cooks make for themselves.

This version uses a lemon, garlic and herb marinade that tastes like summer, and a cooking method that solves the two classic problems with grilled chicken: burnt skin and undercooked meat near the bone.

Why thighs win on the grill

Dark meat contains more fat and connective tissue than white meat. That connective tissue softens as it heats, which means thighs actually improve as they climb past the minimum safe temperature. USDA lists 165°F as safe for all poultry, but thighs are at their best somewhere around 175 to 190°F, when the collagen has melted and the meat turns silky. Breasts, by contrast, start drying out the moment they pass 165°F.

That built-in forgiveness is why thighs are perfect for a charcoal grill, where heat is never perfectly even.

The marinade

For 8 bone-in, skin-on thighs (about 3 pounds):

  • Zest of 2 lemons and juice of 1
  • 4 cloves garlic, grated
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh rosemary or 2 teaspoons dried
  • 1 tablespoon chopped fresh thyme or 1 teaspoon dried
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 2 1/2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

Whisk everything together. The mustard emulsifies the oil and acid so the marinade clings to the meat instead of sliding off.

Notice there is only the juice of one lemon. Too much acid for too long turns chicken surfaces mushy and chalky. The zest carries most of the lemon flavor without that risk. Save the second lemon to squeeze over the finished chicken.

Prep the thighs

Trim any large flaps of loose skin and excess fat hanging off the edges; they cause flare-ups and do not crisp. Leave the skin covering the top of the thigh intact.

With a sharp knife, make two shallow slashes through the skin into the meat on the skin side. This helps the marinade penetrate and lets fat render out of the skin.

Put the thighs in a zip-top bag or a shallow dish, pour in the marinade and massage it into every crevice, especially under the skin. Refrigerate 4 to 12 hours. Even one hour helps if that is all you have.

Setting up the fire

You are going to cook these with a two-zone fire, starting on the cool side and finishing over the coals. That order keeps the skin from burning before the inside is done.

Light a full chimney of charcoal. When the coals are covered in gray ash, pour them into one half of the grill. On the Slot Grill Full Size there is enough room for all eight thighs on the cool side, arranged in two rows. On the Compact, cook four to five at a time or cut the recipe in half.

Let the grate heat for five minutes and then brush it clean. Then grab a paper towel with tongs, dip it in oil and swipe the bars.

Cooking

  1. Take the thighs out of the marinade and let excess drip off. Scrape off big bits of garlic and herbs, which will burn.
  2. Lay the thighs on the side without coals, skin facing up, with the thicker end facing the coals.
  3. Cook without moving for about 20 minutes. Fat will slowly render and the meat will start to firm up.
  4. Turn them over so the skin faces the grate, keeping them off the coals, and cook another 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. Check the thickest part next to the bone with an instant-read thermometer. When it reads about 170°F, move the thighs skin-side down over the coals.
  6. Crisp the skin for 2 to 4 minutes, watching closely. If the fat flares, move the piece back to the cool side for a moment.
  7. Flip once more and give the bone side a minute of direct heat. Pull when the meat by the bone reads 175 to 185°F.

Total time is usually 35 to 45 minutes. The skin should be deep golden brown and blistered, and the juices should run clear.

Why this order beats the alternative

The usual approach is to start chicken over direct heat to get grill marks, then move it to the cool side to finish. The problem is that chicken skin is loaded with fat. Put cold, fatty skin over hot coals and the fat drips immediately, sending flames up that blacken the outside long before the meat near the bone is safe.

Starting on the cool side renders most of that fat gently. By the time the thighs move over the fire, there is far less to drip, so flare-ups are minimal and the skin simply crisps.

The finishing touch

Right when the thighs come off, squeeze the reserved lemon over them and scatter fresh chopped parsley and a little flaky salt. Let them rest five minutes. The warm meat absorbs the fresh juice and the flavor brightens dramatically.

What to serve with them

  • Grilled lemon halves: cut extra lemons in half and grill cut-side down for a minute until caramelized. They are sweeter and juicier than raw.
  • Herbed couscous or orzo dressed with olive oil, lemon, feta and cucumbers.
  • Charred zucchini: halve lengthwise, score, brush with oil and grill directly over the coals for four minutes per side.
  • Tzatziki: grated cucumber squeezed dry, Greek yogurt, garlic, dill, lemon and salt.
  • Warm flatbread tossed on the grate for 30 seconds per side at the end.

Variations on the same method

The technique stays the same; only the marinade changes.

Spicy honey: swap the herbs for 2 tablespoons chipotle in adobo, double the honey, and finish with lime instead of lemon.

Greek-style: add a heaping teaspoon of dried oregano, a tablespoon of red wine vinegar and lots of black pepper. Serve with feta and olives.

Soy-ginger: replace the lemon, herbs and mustard with soy sauce, grated ginger, rice vinegar, sesame oil and a spoonful of brown sugar. Because of the sugar, keep the final direct-heat step very short.

Yogurt-spice: marinate in plain yogurt with garam masala, cumin, turmeric, garlic and ginger. Yogurt tenderizes gently and gives a beautiful charred crust.

Boneless thighs

Boneless, skinless thighs cook much faster, around 10 to 14 minutes total, and they can go straight over medium direct heat since there is no skin to burn. Flip every two or three minutes and pull at 175°F. They are excellent for tacos, rice bowls and salads.

Leftovers that are better than the original

Grilled thighs keep for three to four days in the refrigerator. Pull the meat from the bone and use it in:

  • Chicken salad with grilled corn and avocado
  • Quesadillas on a griddle with pepper jack and pickled jalapenos
  • Fried rice with scallions and a fried egg
  • A quick soup with white beans, kale and chicken stock

Food safety reminders

Cooked chicken always goes onto a clean plate, never the one that carried it raw. Discard used marinade or boil it hard for a full minute before using it as a sauce. Wash tongs that touched raw meat before using them on cooked meat, or keep a second pair. Check temperature in more than one thigh, since pieces on the edge of the fire will cook at different speeds.

Doing it on a small grill

A compact charcoal grill handles this recipe beautifully as long as you respect the two-zone layout. Use about two-thirds of a chimney of charcoal, keep the coals tight against one end, and cook in batches if needed. Hold finished thighs on a sheet pan tented loosely with foil; the skin stays crisp for 15 minutes or so.

The takeaway

Low first, high last. Thighs reward that patience with skin that crackles and meat that falls from the bone. Once you have this rhythm down, chicken night on the grill stops being stressful and becomes the easiest cook of the week.

Quick questions

What temperature should grilled chicken thighs reach?

USDA's safe minimum is 165°F, but thighs taste best around 175 to 185°F, when their connective tissue has softened.

Why do my chicken thighs flare up?

Fat dripping from the skin onto hot coals. Start on the cool side to render fat before moving over direct heat.

How long can chicken marinate in lemon?

With the juice of one lemon in the marinade, 4 to 12 hours is ideal. Longer and the acid can make the surface mushy.
