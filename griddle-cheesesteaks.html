Shaved Ribeye Cheesesteaks on the Griddle

Paper-thin beef, sweet onions and gooey cheese folded together on hot steel and stuffed into a toasted roll. A sandwich worth building a fire for.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

A cheesesteak looks simple: beef, onions, cheese and bread. But the difference between a great one and a sad, chewy one comes down to a handful of details. How thin is the beef? How hot is the surface? When does the cheese go on? What kind of roll? This guide walks through every decision so you can make a cheesesteak on your griddle that is juicy, beefy, and wrapped in molten cheese.

The beef: ribeye, sliced very thin

Ribeye is the classic and best choice because its marbling melts as it cooks, keeping the thin shavings juicy. Sirloin is a leaner, less expensive alternative that still works if sliced thin enough.

The key is slicing. The beef should be shaved almost paper thin, about 1/16 of an inch. Here is how to do it at home:

  1. Trim any thick fat cap or gristle.
  2. Wrap the steak tightly in plastic and freeze for 45 to 60 minutes, until firm but not frozen solid.
  3. Using your sharpest knife, slice across the grain as thinly as you possibly can.

Many grocery stores and butchers also sell pre-shaved beef for sandwiches, or will slice a ribeye for you on their slicer if you ask.

Plan on 6 to 8 ounces of beef per sandwich. A 2-pound ribeye makes four generous cheesesteaks.

The cheese debate

There are three classic options, and all are correct:

  • Provolone: mild or sharp, sliced. It melts into the beef and adds a slight tang. A popular choice.
  • American: melts beautifully and creates a creamy, cohesive sandwich.
  • Cheese sauce: a warm, pourable processed cheese sauce is iconic. You can make your own by melting 8 ounces of American cheese with 1/2 cup milk and a pinch of mustard powder in a small saucepan.

Use 2 to 3 slices per sandwich, or a generous ladle of sauce.

The roll

Use a long, soft-yet-sturdy hoagie roll with a slightly crisp crust and a fluffy interior. It needs to be strong enough to hold the juicy filling without falling apart, but soft enough to bite through. Avoid crusty baguettes, which are too hard. Slice the rolls lengthwise but leave one side attached like a hinge.

Optional add-ons

  • Sliced green bell peppers
  • Sautéed mushrooms
  • Hot or sweet cherry peppers
  • Pickled banana peppers
  • A swipe of mayonnaise, ketchup or hot sauce on the roll

Setting up the griddle

You need a hot, wide flat surface for the beef to sear quickly in a thin layer. Light a full chimney of charcoal and spread it under the griddle with one zone hotter than the other. Let the steel heat for 10 to 15 minutes.

Cooking

Onions first

Slice 2 large yellow onions thinly. Add a tablespoon of oil to the medium zone and cook the onions, stirring occasionally, until soft and golden, about 10 minutes. Season with salt. If you are adding peppers or mushrooms, cook them alongside. Push them to the warm side.

Toast the rolls

Brush the cut side of each roll with butter or a little oil and toast face-down for 30 to 60 seconds. You want light color and crispness on the inside, which helps keep the bread from going soggy. Set aside.

The beef

  1. Add a tablespoon of oil to the hottest zone.
  2. Spread the shaved beef in a thin layer. Season generously with salt and pepper. Do not crowd it; cook in batches if necessary.
  3. Let it sear without moving for about 1 minute, until browned on the bottom.
  4. Use two spatulas to flip, separate and chop the beef into smaller pieces. Cook another 1 to 2 minutes until barely pink.
  5. Mix the onions (and peppers, if using) into the beef.

The cheese melt

Divide the beef and onion mixture into piles, each roughly the length of your roll. Lay cheese slices over the top of each pile. Let the cheese melt for 30 seconds. To speed melting, splash a teaspoon of water near the pile and cover with a metal bowl or lid; the steam will melt the cheese instantly.

The scoop

Here is the classic move: lay the open roll face-down on top of the cheesy beef pile. Slide a long spatula under the beef and, in one motion, flip the whole thing over so the beef ends up inside the roll. It takes a little practice but it looks great and keeps everything neat.

If using cheese sauce, pile the beef into the roll and ladle the warm sauce over the top.

Serving

Cheesesteaks are best eaten immediately while the cheese is gooey. Wrap each one halfway in foil or parchment to hold it together. Serve with:

  • French fries or potato chips
  • Pickles and pickled peppers
  • A crisp green salad
  • Cold drinks

Variations

Chicken cheesesteak: substitute thinly sliced boneless chicken thighs. Cook until no longer pink, about 4 to 5 minutes.

Pizza steak: top the beef with marinara and mozzarella.

Mushroom cheesesteak: replace half or all of the beef with sliced cremini and portobello mushrooms cooked until browned, with soy sauce and garlic.

Breakfast cheesesteak: add scrambled eggs and hash browns to the roll with the beef.

Southwest: add roasted poblano peppers, jalapeños and pepper jack, with a lime-chipotle mayo.

Making it for a crowd

Cheesesteaks are great for game days and parties because the components can be prepped ahead. Slice the beef and onions the day before and store them separately in the refrigerator. Cook the onions in advance and keep them warm on the edge of the griddle. Then cook the beef in batches, building sandwiches assembly-line style. A full griddle on the Full Size can handle three or four sandwiches at once.

Tips from the flat-top

  • Use two spatulas. Chopping and folding the beef with two sharp spatulas makes the work faster and more even.
  • Season aggressively. Thin beef needs more salt than you think.
  • Do not overcook. Thinly sliced beef cooks in a couple of minutes and dries out quickly.
  • Keep it moving. Toss and chop so every piece gets a little browning and none of it burns.
  • Scrape between batches. Remove browned bits so the next batch does not stick.

Cleaning up

While the griddle is still hot, scrape off cheese, onion bits and grease. Squirt a little water on stuck-on cheese and scrape as it steams. Wipe with paper towels and finish with a thin coat of oil to protect the steel.

The takeaway

A great cheesesteak is all about thin beef, a hot griddle and well-timed cheese. Once you have the technique down, you will be making them for every game day and lazy Saturday. The flip-and-scoop takes practice, but even your messy attempts will taste spectacular.

Going further: cheesesteak details

Slicing beef thin

Freeze the ribeye for 30 to 45 minutes until firm but not hard, then slice as thinly as possible with a sharp knife. Thin slices cook in a minute or two on a hot griddle and stay tender.

Choose your cheese

Provolone melts cleanly and adds a mild tang. American cheese gives a creamy, gooey texture. A homemade cheese sauce works if you want it extra rich.

Toast the rolls

Split the rolls and toast them cut side down on the griddle with a little butter for a crisp interior that holds up to the juices.

Building on a split surface

On a grill with one griddle plate and one grate, you can toast rolls and onions on the griddle while charring peppers over the grate. Then move everything to the griddle to finish.

Quick questions

What cut of beef is best for cheesesteaks?

Ribeye, sliced very thin. Its marbling keeps the shaved beef juicy.

How do I slice beef thin at home?

Freeze it for 45 to 60 minutes until firm, then slice across the grain with a very sharp knife.

Which cheese goes on a cheesesteak?

Provolone, American or a warm cheese sauce are all classic choices.
