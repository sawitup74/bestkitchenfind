Cooking for a Crowd on One Grill

How to feed 15, 20 or 30 people from a single charcoal grill without long lines, burnt food or a frazzled cook.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

At some point every backyard cook faces the big one: the graduation party, the family reunion, the neighborhood block party. Suddenly you need to feed 20 or 30 people from one grill. It can feel impossible. It is not. With a smart menu and good workflow, one grill can feed a big crowd and keep the food coming hot and fast.

Rule one: choose foods with high throughput

Some foods cook fast and take up little space. Others take forever or need a lot of room. For crowds, favor:

  • Sausages and hot dogs: cook quickly, fit tightly, hold well.
  • Smash burgers or sliders: cook in minutes.
  • Chicken thighs (boneless): 10 to 14 minutes over direct heat.
  • Skewers: cook fast, fit efficiently and portion themselves.
  • Shrimp: minutes to cook.
  • Pork tenderloin: 25 minutes, slices into many portions.
  • Flank or skirt steak: cooks in 10 minutes, slices thin to serve many.

Avoid making many individual thick steaks or whole chickens unless you have the time and space.

Rule two: cook ahead and hold

Proteins that hold well can be cooked in advance and kept warm:

  • Pulled pork or shredded chicken: cook at home the day before, reheat on the grill.
  • Bone-in chicken: grill early, hold in a covered foil pan.
  • Sausages: grill, then hold in a pan of warm beer and onions.

The faux cambro

Restaurants hold hot food in insulated boxes. At home, an empty cooler works the same way. Line it with towels, add foil-wrapped cooked meat, and close it. Food will stay hot and safe (above 140°F) for one to two hours. Check with a thermometer.

Rule three: make sides that do not need the grill

Choose sides that can be made ahead and served cold or at room temperature: pasta salad, potato salad, coleslaw, grain salads, green salads, fruit, chips and dips. Use the grill only for one or two sides, such as corn or vegetables.

Rule four: maximize grill space

Organize the cooking area

On a rectangular grill, divide the cooking area into lanes:

  • Hot lane: for searing and fast cooking.
  • Medium lane: for cooking through.
  • Holding lane: at the cool end, for finished food waiting to be served.

Use a griddle

Use pans

Foil pans on the grill can hold and reheat sides, beans and pulled meats without taking up much space.

Rule five: manage the fire for a long cook

Feeding a crowd can take two or three hours. Plan your fuel:

  • Have at least double the charcoal you think you need.
  • Add fresh charcoal to the hot zone every 30 to 45 minutes.
  • Keep a second chimney lit and ready if you need to boost heat quickly.

Rule six: cook in waves

Do not try to cook everything at once. Serve food in waves:

  1. First wave: snacks and appetizers as guests arrive.
  2. Second wave: the first batch of main proteins and sides.
  3. Third wave: a second batch of proteins for latecomers and seconds.
  4. Final wave: dessert from the dying coals.

Waves keep food hot and fresh and prevent long lines at the grill.

Rule seven: get help

  • Assign roles. One person grills, one assembles and plates, one manages the food table.
  • Set up self-serve stations. Let guests build their own burgers, tacos or plates.
  • Recruit a runner to carry food from the grill to the table.

Timing a big cook

A rough timeline for a crowd of 20 to 30:

  • Day before: marinate, make sides, form patties, prep skewers.
  • 3 hours before: set up tables, coolers, shade.
  • 2 hours before: light the grill. Cook anything that holds well, like chicken thighs.
  • 1 hour before: guests arrive, put out snacks.
  • Mealtime: cook fast items in waves.
  • After: dessert on the dying coals.

Food safety for crowds

Large gatherings carry more food safety risk because food sits out longer.

  • Use ice baths under cold foods.
  • Hold hot food above 140°F.
  • Throw out perishables left out more than two hours (one hour above 90°F).
  • Use separate tongs and plates for raw and cooked meat.
  • Check every batch with a thermometer.

Cleanup strategy

  • Put out plenty of labeled trash and recycling bins.
  • Have a dish tub of soapy water for utensils.
  • Package leftovers in zip-top bags for guests to take home.
  • Clean the grill while it is still warm.

The takeaway

Feeding a crowd from one grill is about smart choices: high-throughput foods, make-ahead sides, holding techniques, organized grill space and cooking in waves. With a plan and a little help, you can feed a crowd and still have time to enjoy the party.

Holding food safely

Cooked food should be kept hot (140°F or above) or served promptly. A cooler with no ice, lined with towels, makes an excellent insulated holding box for wrapped grilled meats for up to an hour. Check temperatures with a thermometer.

Recruit help

Give guests jobs: a drinks captain, a side-dish captain, a cleanup crew. The cook should focus on the fire.

Feeding fire efficiently

Keep a lit chimney going on a safe, non-flammable surface beside the grill so you can add hot coals when the fire weakens. Adding unlit coals directly can cause smoke and temperature dips. On grills where the grates lift away, such as the two-piece grates on the Full Size Slot Grill, you can add coal without moving all of the food.

Buffet layout

Set the buffet in the order guests eat: plates, buns, proteins, toppings, sides, sauces, napkins and utensils at the end. Keep raw meat far from cooked food and serving areas.

Cook's notebook

Menu choices that make one grill enough

Pick foods that cook in batches and hold well. Sausages, chicken thighs, and kebabs tolerate a little waiting on the cool side without drying out, while thin fish fillets and well-done burgers do not. A whole spatchcocked chicken occupies the cool side for most of an hour without attention, freeing the hot side for quick bites. Build at least half the menu from sides that need no grill at all. And when the crowd is truly huge, consider a flat-top: two griddle plates turn a Full Size Slot Grill into a continuous cooking surface for smash burgers, which take two or three minutes each and can be stacked onto toasted buns in an assembly line. Speed wins at big parties, and simple, fast food keeps the cook out of the weeds.

Quick questions

How many burgers can I cook at once?

On a grate of about 231 square inches, roughly 10 to 12 standard patties; more as smash burgers on a griddle.

How do I keep grilled food hot for a party?

Wrap it in foil and hold it in a towel-lined empty cooler; it stays hot for one to two hours.

How much fuel should I buy for an all-afternoon party?

Plan on double what you think, adding fresh coals every 30 to 45 minutes.
