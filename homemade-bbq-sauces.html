Homemade Barbecue Sauces: Five Regional-Style Recipes

Sweet and tangy tomato, vinegar-pepper, mustard gold, white sauce and a spicy-sweet glaze, plus when and how to sauce on the grill.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Barbecue sauce is one of the most personal parts of an outdoor cookout. Families defend their recipes, regions argue about styles, and every backyard cook eventually tinkers with a signature version. The good news: homemade sauce is easy, cheap and better than most bottled versions. Here are five styles inspired by American regional barbecue and tips for using them.

The building blocks

Most barbecue sauces balance a few flavors:

  • Sweet: brown sugar, molasses, honey, maple syrup
  • Sour: vinegar, citrus
  • Savory: tomato, Worcestershire sauce, onion, garlic
  • Heat: black pepper, cayenne, chili flakes, hot sauce
  • Smoke: smoked paprika, chipotle

Tweaking the balance creates a new sauce.

Sauce 1: Sweet and tangy tomato sauce

The thick, sticky style that most people think of as barbecue sauce. Great on ribs, chicken and pulled pork.

  • 1 1/2 cups ketchup
  • 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/3 cup brown sugar
  • 2 tablespoons molasses
  • 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tablespoon Dijon or yellow mustard
  • 1 tablespoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne

Whisk everything in a saucepan and simmer over low heat, stirring often, for 15 to 20 minutes until slightly thickened.

Sauce 2: Vinegar-pepper sauce

Thin, sharp and spicy, inspired by Eastern Carolina barbecue. It cuts through fatty pork beautifully.

  • 1 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/2 cup white vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon crushed red pepper flakes
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne

Shake together in a jar. Let it sit for a few hours or overnight before using. No cooking needed.

Sauce 3: Mustard gold sauce

A tangy, golden sauce inspired by South Carolina, perfect with pork and chicken.

  • 3/4 cup yellow mustard
  • 1/4 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1/4 cup honey
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 teaspoon hot sauce
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Simmer for 5 to 10 minutes.

Sauce 4: Alabama-style white sauce

A creamy, peppery mayonnaise-based sauce for chicken and turkey.

  • 1 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/4 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon prepared horseradish
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons coarsely ground black pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne

Whisk together. Refrigerate for an hour to let the flavors combine. Use it to baste chicken at the end of cooking and as a dipping sauce.

Sauce 5: Spicy-sweet glaze

A sticky, shiny glaze for wings, pork chops and salmon.

  • 1/2 cup honey
  • 1/4 cup hot sauce
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
  • 2 cloves garlic, grated

Simmer for 5 minutes until glossy.

When to sauce

Most tomato-based and sweet sauces contain sugar, which burns quickly over high heat. Follow these rules:

  • Sauce at the end. Brush on sweet sauces in the last 5 to 10 minutes of cooking.
  • Use the cooler side. Move food to the indirect side of a two-zone fire while saucing.
  • Build layers. Apply thin coats, letting each one set for a few minutes before adding another.
  • Serve extra on the side.

Vinegar sauces and white sauce have less sugar and can be used earlier, as mops or bastes.

Storage

  • Tomato and mustard sauces keep about a month in the refrigerator.
  • Vinegar sauce keeps for several months refrigerated.
  • White sauce keeps about a week because of the mayonnaise.
  • Glaze keeps about two weeks.

Always use clean jars and utensils.

Make it your own

  • Add bourbon or a splash of coffee to the tomato sauce.
  • Swap honey for maple syrup.
  • Add chipotle for smoky heat.
  • Use fruit, such as peach preserves or cherry jam, for a fruity sauce.
  • Add fresh grated ginger for an Asian twist.

Saucing on a flat-top

Saucing on a griddle is easy and lets you caramelize sauces without flare-ups. Toss cooked chicken or shrimp in a glaze on the hot plate for the last minute, or brush sauce on burgers right before they come off.

The takeaway

Homemade barbecue sauce is fast, cheap and endlessly adaptable. Try all five styles, find your favorite, and remember the golden rule: sweet sauces go on at the end.

Going further: a sauce maker's notebook

Taste in three passes

Sauce changes as it simmers and again as it cools. Taste once when everything is whisked together, once after ten minutes on the stove and once more after it has cooled to room temperature. A sauce that seems sharp and hot in the pan often rounds out at the table, and a sauce that tastes perfectly balanced while hot can turn flat and sugary when cold. Make your final adjustments on that last, cool taste.

Fixing a sauce that went sideways

  • Too sweet: a teaspoon of vinegar at a time, plus a pinch of salt. Salt makes sweetness read as fuller instead of cloying.
  • Too sour: a spoon of brown sugar or honey, or a knob of butter to soften the edges.
  • Too thin: keep simmering, uncovered, stirring often. It thickens more once chilled, so stop a little before you think it is ready.
  • Too thick: loosen with water, apple juice or a splash of beer.
  • Too hot: more of every other ingredient. Heat cannot be removed, only diluted.
  • Flat: almost always needs salt or acid. Add a tiny amount of each and taste again.

Simmer safety

Thick tomato sauces bubble and spit like lava. Use a deep pot, keep the heat low, and stir with a long spoon. Partly covering the pot with a lid set ajar cuts the splatter without trapping all the steam.

Saucing over charcoal without the soot

A sweet sauce dripping onto lit coals flares up and coats food with bitter black specks. Keep the food over the cool side of the grill when you brush, keep your brush lightly loaded, and let each coat tack up for two or three minutes before the next. If you want a lacquered crust, finish with one quick pass over the coals, turning constantly, for no more than thirty seconds a side.

Carbon steel and acidic sauces

Long contact with vinegar or tomato can soften a young layer of seasoning on carbon steel. That is no problem for normal saucing, but avoid leaving puddles of sauce sitting on a griddle plate after the cook. Scrape, wipe and re-oil while the steel is still warm, and a fresh layer of seasoning will keep building every time you cook.

Batching for the season

Double the tomato and mustard recipes at the start of summer and freeze them in pint containers, leaving headroom for expansion. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and whisk before using. Vinegar sauce does not need freezing at all; it keeps for months in the fridge and only gets better.

Sauce as a gift

Bottled sauce is one of the easiest homemade gifts. Ladle hot tomato or mustard sauce into clean jars, cool, label with the date and keep refrigerated. Tell the recipient to use it within a month and to treat it like fresh food, not a shelf-stable product.

A sauce tasting night

For a fun cookout, grill plain chicken thighs and pork chops with only salt and pepper, then set out all five sauces in small bowls with labels. Guests taste each with a bite of meat and vote. It is a cheap, easy party, and you will find out which recipe deserves a permanent spot in the refrigerator door.

Start here

Quick questions

When should I put sauce on ribs or chicken?

In the last 5 to 10 minutes, ideally over indirect heat, so the sugar does not burn.

How long does homemade barbecue sauce keep?

Tomato-based sauces keep about a month refrigerated; white sauce about a week.

What is Alabama white sauce?

A tangy, peppery mayonnaise-based sauce, traditionally served with chicken.

Can I freeze homemade barbecue sauce?

Tomato and mustard sauces freeze well for several months. Thaw in the refrigerator and whisk before using.

Why does my sauce turn black on the grill?

Sugar is burning. Sauce over indirect heat and only in the final minutes.
