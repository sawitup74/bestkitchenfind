Crispy Griddle Quesadillas and Breakfast Tacos

Two flat-top staples that feed a hungry crowd fast: lacy cheese-crusted quesadillas and soft, loaded breakfast tacos.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

If you own a griddle, these two dishes will become your most-cooked recipes. Quesadillas and breakfast tacos are cheap, endlessly customizable, kid-approved and ready in minutes. They are also perfect proof of why a flat steel surface is so useful: you can warm tortillas, crisp cheese, scramble eggs and sear fillings all in one place. Here is how to make both like a taqueria.

The tortilla question

Flour tortillas are the standard for quesadillas and most breakfast tacos. They are soft, pliable and fold without cracking. Look for fresh, thick tortillas, or the uncooked tortillas sold in refrigerated packs that you cook yourself on the griddle; they puff into flaky, fresh-tasting rounds.

Corn tortillas have a deeper, toasty corn flavor and make wonderful small tacos. They need to be heated thoroughly or they crack. Use two per taco for structure.

Store tortillas sealed tightly; dry tortillas tear and crack.

Griddle setup

Light a full chimney of charcoal and spread it under the griddle with one side a little thinner for medium heat. Most of this cooking happens at medium. Too much heat burns tortillas before the cheese melts.

The perfect quesadilla

Choose the right cheese

You need a cheese that melts smoothly and stretches. The best choices:

  • Oaxaca cheese: a stringy, mild Mexican cheese that melts beautifully.
  • Monterey Jack: creamy and mild.
  • Pepper jack: Monterey Jack with a kick.
  • Mozzarella: stretchy and mild.
  • Medium cheddar: more flavor, slightly greasier melt.

A mix of two cheeses is ideal. Shred your own from a block; pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking agents that keep it from melting as smoothly.

Fillings

Keep fillings modest; overstuffed quesadillas fall apart and the cheese will not melt through. Good fillings include:

  • Shredded grilled chicken with roasted poblanos
  • Grilled steak with caramelized onions
  • Sautéed mushrooms with spinach and garlic
  • Black beans, corn and pickled jalapeños
  • Chorizo and potato
  • Squash blossoms or zucchini with epazote

All fillings should be fully cooked and warm before going into the quesadilla.

Method

  1. Lay a flour tortilla on the medium-heat zone.
  2. Sprinkle a thin layer of cheese over the whole tortilla.
  3. Add your fillings to one half.
  4. Add another layer of cheese over the fillings.
  5. When the cheese starts to melt, fold the empty half over the filled half. Press gently with a spatula.
  6. Cook 1 to 2 minutes until golden, then flip and cook another 1 to 2 minutes.
  7. Transfer to a board, rest for 1 minute so the cheese sets, then cut into wedges.

The lacy cheese crust trick

For the most addictive quesadilla, add a "frico" crust: sprinkle a small handful of shredded cheese directly onto the hot griddle in the shape of the tortilla, then place the filled quesadilla on top. The cheese fries into a crisp, golden lace that sticks to the outside of the tortilla. Flip and repeat on the other side. Use a firm, flavorful cheese like cheddar or a blend with a little parmesan.

Breakfast tacos for a crowd

Breakfast tacos are a morning institution in Texas and the Southwest for good reason: soft tortillas wrapped around fluffy eggs and fillings, topped with salsa, eaten on the go.

Classic fillings

  • Bacon, egg and cheese
  • Potato and egg: diced potatoes griddled with onions until crispy
  • Chorizo and egg: crumbled Mexican chorizo cooked until crispy, with scrambled eggs
  • Migas: scrambled eggs with fried tortilla strips, onions, peppers, tomato and cheese
  • Bean and cheese: refried beans with shredded cheese
  • Sausage, egg and cheese

Griddle potatoes

Dice 1.5 pounds of potatoes into 1/2-inch cubes. Microwave or parboil until just tender, about 5 minutes, then drain well. Spread on an oiled, hot griddle and cook without stirring for 5 minutes until browned. Toss, add a diced onion and cook another 5 to 8 minutes until crisp. Season with salt, pepper, garlic powder and a pinch of chili powder.

Fluffy scrambled eggs for tacos

Whisk 12 eggs with a pinch of salt and 1/4 cup milk. Pour into a buttered area of the griddle at medium-low heat. Let them sit for 20 seconds, then push them gently into large curds with a spatula. Keep pushing and folding until they are just set but still glossy. They will continue to cook from residual heat.

Building a taco station

  1. Cook bacon, chorizo or sausage first, then potatoes in the rendered fat.
  2. Keep cooked fillings warm on the cooler edge of the griddle or in foil pans.
  3. Cook eggs last.
  4. Warm tortillas on the griddle for 15 to 30 seconds per side and wrap them in a towel.
  5. Set out salsas, cheese, avocado, cilantro and hot sauces.

Salsas

Salsa roja

Char 4 plum tomatoes, 2 jalapeños, 1/2 onion and 3 unpeeled garlic cloves on the grate until blackened in spots. Peel the garlic. Blend everything with a handful of cilantro, juice of 1 lime and salt until smooth.

Salsa verde

Husk and rinse 1 pound of tomatillos. Char on the grate with 2 serrano peppers and 1/2 onion. Blend with cilantro, lime juice and salt.

Pico de gallo

Dice tomatoes, white onion, jalapeño and cilantro. Toss with lime juice and salt.

Kid-friendly versions

Kids love quesadillas and breakfast tacos. Keep fillings simple: cheese only, or cheese with plain shredded chicken. Cut quesadillas into small triangles. Offer mild salsa, sour cream and guacamole for dipping.

Make-ahead and freezer-friendly

Breakfast tacos freeze surprisingly well. Assemble tacos with eggs, potatoes, cheese and meat, wrap each tightly in foil, and freeze in a zip-top bag for up to two months. Reheat in the foil on the griddle or in a 350°F oven until heated through, about 20 minutes from frozen. Add fresh salsa after reheating.

Quesadillas can be assembled a few hours ahead, stacked between parchment, and refrigerated until it is time to cook.

Troubleshooting

The tortilla burned before the cheese melted. The heat was too high. Cook on medium, or cover the quesadilla with a metal bowl for 30 seconds to trap heat and speed the melt.

The quesadilla fell apart. It was overstuffed. Use less filling and more cheese, which acts as glue.

The tacos were soggy. Wet fillings or salsa added too early. Drain fillings and add salsa just before eating.

Corn tortillas cracked. They were not warm enough. Heat them on the griddle until soft and pliable, or briefly dampen your hands and pat the tortillas before heating.

Cleanup

Scrape off burnt cheese while the griddle is still hot. A bit of water and a scraper lift stubborn spots. Wipe dry and apply a very thin coat of oil.

The takeaway

Quesadillas and breakfast tacos are the workhorses of griddle cooking: fast, cheap, and loved by everyone. Keep tortillas, eggs and cheese on hand, and you will always be ten minutes away from a crowd-pleasing meal.

Going further: tortilla tips

Warm tortillas properly

Warm corn tortillas on a dry, hot griddle for 20 to 30 seconds per side until soft and spotted. Stack them in a towel to keep them warm and pliable. Flour tortillas need only a few seconds per side.

Don't overfill quesadillas

Too much filling makes quesadillas hard to flip and leaves the cheese unmelted. Use a thin layer and press gently with a spatula.

Breakfast taco bar

Scramble eggs, fry potatoes and crisp chorizo on the griddle, then set them out with warm tortillas, salsa, cheese and avocado. Everyone builds their own.

Cook's notebook

Keep them crisp

Serve quesadillas right away, cut into wedges with a pizza cutter. If you are making a big batch, hold finished ones on a wire rack in a warm oven rather than stacking them, which steams them soft. Brush the outside of each tortilla with a little butter or oil for a golden, blistered finish.

Quick questions

What cheese melts best for quesadillas?

Oaxaca, Monterey Jack, pepper jack and mozzarella melt smoothly. Shred from a block for the best melt.

Can breakfast tacos be frozen?

Yes. Wrap assembled tacos in foil, freeze up to two months, and reheat in the foil.

What heat should I use for quesadillas?

Medium. High heat burns the tortilla before the cheese melts.
