Great Gifts Under $30 for Anyone Who Grills

Stocking stuffers, secret santa winners and thank-you gifts that cooks will actually use, plus homemade ideas that cost almost nothing.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Not every great gift has to be big. Some of the most appreciated presents for cooks are small, practical tools and treats that make every cook a little better. Whether you are filling a stocking, shopping for a secret santa exchange or thanking a neighbor, here are gifts under $30 that people who grill will truly use.

The rules of a great small gift

  • It gets used often. A tool reached for every cook beats a gadget used once.
  • It is better than what they have. An upgrade over a basic version.
  • It is consumable or compact. Small gifts should not take up a lot of space.
  • It feels thoughtful. A personal touch matters.

Homemade gifts

Homemade gifts are personal, inexpensive and often more appreciated than store-bought ones.

Signature spice rub

Mix a big batch of rub and divide it into small jars. A versatile all-purpose rub:

  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup smoked paprika
  • 2 tablespoons kosher salt
  • 1 tablespoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon garlic powder
  • 1 tablespoon onion powder
  • 2 teaspoons chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon dry mustard
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne

Label each jar with the name of the rub, the ingredients, and suggested uses.

Finishing salts

Flavor coarse sea salt with herbs, citrus or spices:

  • Rosemary-lemon salt: mix 1/2 cup flaky salt with 1 tablespoon finely chopped fresh rosemary and the zest of 1 lemon. Spread on a sheet pan to dry for a day.
  • Smoked chili salt: mix salt with smoked paprika and chili flakes.
  • Garlic-herb salt: mix with dried garlic, thyme and oregano.

Homemade barbecue sauce

Simmer ketchup, apple cider vinegar, brown sugar, molasses, Worcestershire sauce, mustard, smoked paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, salt and pepper for 20 minutes. Bottle in clean jars. Refrigerate and use within a month.

Chili crisp

Heat neutral oil with sliced shallots and garlic until crisp, then pour it over a mix of chili flakes, Sichuan peppercorns, sesame seeds, sugar and salt. Jar and refrigerate.

Recipe card set

Write your best grilling recipes on index cards and tie them with string. Include tips and personal notes.

The takeaway

The best gifts for grillers do not have to be expensive. A fast thermometer, a seasoning balm or a jar of homemade rub can make every cook better and remind them of you each time they use it. Choose something practical, add a personal touch, and you have the perfect small gift.

Going further: getting the most from small gifts

How to give a thermometer so it gets used

A thermometer in a drawer helps nobody. Attach a small card with the target temperatures the recipient will use most:

FoodPull temperature
Chicken and turkey165°F
Ground beef160°F
Pork chops and tenderloin145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Steak, medium-rareAbout 130°F after resting
Fish145°F, or until it flakes

These targets follow USDA guidance for safety, with the steak target being a common preference rather than a safety rule. The magnetic back lets the thermometer live on the fridge, right where the recipient will see it.

How to give a seasoning balm so it gets used

Seasoning balm works best with a little instruction. Include a short card:

  1. Clean and dry the steel completely.
  2. Rub on a very thin coat of balm with a lint-free cloth, then wipe off as much as you can.
  3. Heat at 400°F for an hour.
  4. Repeat for a darker, slicker layer.

Many first-time carbon steel owners use too much oil. A note that says "thinner than you think" saves frustration.

Gifts for kids who like to help

Kids who like cooking appreciate small tools of their own: a kid-sized apron, a timer, a recipe card they can follow. Grilling should always be supervised, but kids can measure rubs, brush on sauce, and read the thermometer, which makes them feel like part of the crew.

When a small gift is better than a big one

Small gifts are often the right choice when you do not know someone well, when a group has a price limit, or when the person already owns a grill they love. They are also ideal for gift exchanges where a big-ticket item would feel out of place.

Cook's notebook

A last word on value

Price is only half of value. A small gift earns its keep by how often it gets used. A thermometer may be pulled out every single cook for years, so it costs pennies per use. A tin of balm keeps a grill, a griddle and a skillet in good shape season after season. Homemade rubs disappear fast and invite the recipient to come back and ask for more, which is the best compliment a homemade gift can receive. When you are choosing between two small gifts, picture the recipient on an ordinary Tuesday night. The gift they reach for on that ordinary night is the one to buy. Add a note in your own handwriting, and even a nine-dollar tin feels like a thoughtful present.
