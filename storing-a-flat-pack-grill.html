Carbon Steel Care

Storing a Grill in a Small Home

No patio shed? No garage? How to store a charcoal grill indoors, keep it rust-free, and why flat-pack steel changes the small-space math.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Plenty of people who love to cook do not grill because they think they have nowhere to keep a grill. A traditional kettle or cart grill takes up a few square feet of patio, needs a cover, and slowly weathers in the rain. If you live in an apartment, a condo, a townhouse or a house without much outdoor storage, that bulky footprint is a real problem. This guide covers how to store a grill when space is tight, and how to keep it in good shape between cooks.

The small-space grilling challenge

Grill storage problems usually come down to three things:

  • Space: a full-size grill can occupy 10 or more square feet.
  • Weather: grills left outside rust, fade and collect debris.
  • Rules: many apartments and condos limit what can be stored on balconies or patios.

The solution is either a grill that is small enough to tuck away, or one that comes apart and stores flat.

Storage spots for a flat grill

  • Coat closet: slide it vertically against the side wall.
  • Under the bed: in a fabric bag, it fits easily.
  • Pantry or broom closet: next to sheet pans and cutting boards.
  • Garage shelf or wall: hang plates on hooks or rest them on a shelf.
  • Behind the couch or a door.
  • Car trunk: ready for tailgates, picnics and road trips.
  • Under a kitchen cabinet if your cabinets are deep enough.

Before you store: clean and cool

Never store a grill that is still warm or has ash in it.

  1. Let the grill and coals cool completely. Coals can stay hot for many hours.
  2. Dump the cold ash into a metal container.
  3. Scrape the grates and griddle.
  4. Wipe all surfaces with a paper towel.
  5. Wipe the cooking surfaces with the faintest film of oil or balm.
  6. Disassemble and stack the plates.

Preventing mess indoors

Grills carry soot, ash and grease. To keep your home clean:

  • Use a storage bag. A heavy canvas tote, duffel or dedicated grill bag keeps soot contained.
  • Wrap the plates. Old towels, newspaper or kraft paper between plates prevents scratching and absorbs residual oil.
  • Wipe the exterior. A damp cloth removes surface soot before storage.
  • Season the outside, too. A light coat of seasoning balm on the body helps keep soot from rubbing off.

Rust prevention in storage

Humidity is the enemy of carbon steel.

  • Store dry. Make sure every piece is completely dry before packing.
  • Avoid sealed containers. Airtight bins can trap moisture and cause rust.
  • Use desiccant packs in the storage bag if you live somewhere humid.
  • Check monthly. If you see rust, wipe it off and re-oil.
  • Avoid bathrooms, laundry rooms and damp basements.

Carrying and transport

A flat-pack grill makes a great travel grill. To carry it:

  • Stack the plates and secure them with a strap.
  • Keep them in a canvas bag with charcoal in a separate container.
  • Pack a small kit: chimney starter, tongs, spatula, gloves, paper towels, oil and a thermometer.

At around 14 pounds for the Compact and 27.6 for the Full Size, either one is easy enough to carry from a closet to a balcony, backyard, park or tailgate.

Rules for apartments and condos

Before grilling on a balcony or patio, check the rules. Many fire codes and building policies restrict open-flame and charcoal grills on balconies, especially in multi-unit buildings. Some allow them on ground-level patios but not above. Always check:

  • Your lease or HOA rules
  • Local fire codes
  • Required distances from buildings and railings

If balcony grilling is not allowed, a portable grill still lets you cook in parks, beaches, campgrounds and friends' backyards where it is permitted.

Safety when grilling on small patios

  • Set up on a stable, non-flammable surface.
  • Keep the grill well away from walls, railings, furniture and overhanging branches.
  • Never grill indoors or in an enclosed space; charcoal produces carbon monoxide.
  • A raised grill with an air gap underneath keeps a good share of the heat from reaching whatever is underneath. The Slot Grill Compact has a 2⅞ inches of open air under its charcoal tray, and the Full Size has 3⅝ inches. Still, always use a heat-safe surface.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher or bucket of sand handy.

Organizing your grill kit

Keep all your grilling gear together for quick setups:

  • Chimney starter (can nest inside a bag)
  • Charcoal in a sealed bin
  • Fire starters
  • Long tongs and a spatula
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Heat-resistant gloves
  • Grill brush or scraper
  • Oil or seasoning balm
  • Paper towels
  • A sheet pan for carrying food out

A plastic tote or canvas bag in the closet next to the grill means you can be cooking within 20 minutes of deciding to grill.

Winter storage

If you do not grill in winter:

  1. Deep clean the grill.
  2. Re-season all surfaces with a thin coat.
  3. Dry thoroughly.
  4. Wrap in paper or cloth.
  5. Store in a dry, indoor space.

In spring, give it a quick wash, dry and season before your first cook.

The takeaway

Small spaces do not mean you have to give up cooking with fire. With a flat-pack steel grill and a few good habits, you can store a real charcoal grill in a closet and pull it out whenever the mood strikes. Clean it, dry it, oil it and pack it away: it will be ready for your next cookout.

Going further: seasonal storage routines

End-of-season deep care

Before putting the grill away for winter:

  1. Burn a hot fire to cook off grease.
  2. Let it cool, then remove ash and scrape the grates.
  3. Wash off any stubborn residue with hot water and a scrubber; avoid soaking.
  4. Dry completely, ideally by heating over a small fire or in an oven if it fits.
  5. Give every surface the faintest film of balm or a high-smoke-point oil, covering every surface.
  6. Heat to set the layer if you can.
  7. Stack the pieces and store them in a dry place.

Where to store

  • Indoors in a closet, under a bed or behind furniture. A flat-pack grill that packs to about an inch or so thick slides into spaces no traditional grill would fit.
  • Garage or shed if it stays dry. Keep it off the concrete floor, which can wick moisture.
  • Car trunk for frequent travelers, ideally inside a canvas or other breathable bag.

Avoid moisture traps

Sealed plastic bins can trap moisture and promote rust, especially in humid areas. Use a breathable bag or wrap the pieces in a cotton towel. Add a silica gel packet or two if storing in a humid place.

Spring wake-up

Unpack, inspect for spots of rust, scrub them off and re-season. Light a fire and cook something fatty. The seasoning layer will refresh quickly.

Storing griddle plates

Season griddle plates with a thin coat of oil, then stack them with a sheet of paper towel between to prevent scratches and absorb moisture.

Cook's notebook

A storage checklist

  • Cool completely.
  • Remove all ash.
  • Scrape and wipe.
  • Dry thoroughly.
  • Oil lightly.
  • Store dry and ventilated.
  • Inspect at the start of the next season.
