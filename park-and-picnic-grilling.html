Picnic and Park Grilling Done Right

How to plan, pack and cook a grilled picnic at a park, beach or lake, including rules, menus, gear and leaving no trace.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

There is something special about cooking in the open air of a park, beach or lakeshore. A grilled picnic turns an ordinary afternoon into an event. It does require more planning than a backyard cookout, because you are bringing everything with you. This guide covers everything you need to know.

Check the rules first

Parks and beaches have very different rules about grilling. Before you go:

  • Are personal grills allowed? Some parks only allow their own fixed grills; others permit personal grills in designated areas.
  • Charcoal or gas? Some areas allow only one or the other.
  • Fire restrictions: during dry seasons, many areas impose burn bans that prohibit charcoal grills.
  • Coal disposal: some parks provide hot coal bins; others require you to carry everything out.
  • Reservations: popular picnic areas may require advance booking.
  • Alcohol policies.

Call the park office or check the official website. Rules can change seasonally.

Why bring your own grill?

Park grills are convenient but often dirty, rusty, or already occupied. Bringing your own grill means you know exactly how clean the cooking surface is, and you can set up wherever grilling is permitted.

Choosing a spot

  • Set the grill on a stable, level, non-flammable surface: a picnic table with a heat-safe barrier, a concrete pad, or bare dirt or sand where permitted.
  • Keep it well away from dry grass, leaves, overhanging branches, tents and people.
  • Check the wind direction so smoke does not blow into your group or neighboring picnickers.
  • A raised grill with an air gap beneath it helps reduce heat to the surface below. Still, always place a heat-safe barrier on any table.

Prep everything at home

The less work at the park, the better:

  • Marinate and skewer meat at home.
  • Chop vegetables and pack them in containers.

  • Pack condiments in small jars or squeeze bottles.
  • Pre-measure charcoal.

Food safety away from home

  • Keep raw meat in a separate cooler with plenty of ice, at or below 40°F.
  • Pack meat frozen if traveling far.
  • Bring separate tongs and plates for raw and cooked food.
  • Pack plenty of wipes or water and soap for hand washing.
  • Check cooked food with a thermometer.
  • Do not leave perishables out more than two hours, or one hour above 90°F.

Grilling at the beach

  • Wind can make charcoal burn hotter and faster. Shield the grill from the wind if possible.
  • Sand can get into food. Set up on a solid surface or a board.
  • Salt air can cause steel to rust. Wipe the grill down with fresh water, dry it and oil it when you get home.
  • Never bury hot coals in the sand. They can stay hot for many hours and burn people walking by.

Leave no trace

Leave your picnic spot cleaner than you found it.

  1. Pack out all trash and recycling.
  2. Let coals burn out completely, then douse them thoroughly with water and stir. Only dispose of them in designated hot coal bins, or pack them out in a metal container.
  3. Do not dump ash on the ground, in the sand or in trash cans.
  4. Pick up any dropped food, wrappers or bottle caps.
  5. Leave natural surfaces as you found them.

Cooling and packing the grill

Steel holds heat longer than you might expect. Let the grill cool completely before disassembling and packing it. Carry a heat-safe bag or metal container in case you need to leave before it is fully cool, and never put a warm grill directly into a car.

The takeaway

A grilled picnic takes a little more planning, but the reward is a memorable meal in a beautiful setting. Check the rules, prep at home, bring a portable grill, keep food safe and leave no trace. Then enjoy the view.

Going further: details that make a park cook easy

Read the rules before you pack

Parks set their own rules for open flames. Some allow portable charcoal grills anywhere on the lawn, some allow them only at designated pads, and some ban charcoal entirely during dry spells. Check the park's website or call the office. Fire bans can change week to week in summer, so check again the morning of your outing.

Protect the grass

Even a raised grill radiates heat downward. Set it on a fireproof surface such as a concrete pad, a gravel area, a large flat paver you bring along or a designated grill area. Never set a hot grill directly on dry grass or leaves.

Coal management on the go

  • Light the chimney on a non-flammable surface, away from foot traffic.
  • Use only as much charcoal as you need. A half chimney is often enough for a picnic lunch for four on a Compact.
  • Plan the menu so the hottest cooks come first and the gentle ones, like vegetables or s'mores, come last as the coals fade.

Ash disposal done right

Ashes stay hot for hours. Use a park's designated hot-coal bin if it has one. Otherwise, douse coals thoroughly with water, stir, douse again and confirm that the ashes are cool before packing them into a metal container. Never dump coals in a trash can, on the grass or into the woods.

Packing the hot grill

Carbon steel holds heat. Let the grill cool before taking it apart, or wear thick heat-resistant gloves. A Slot Grill comes apart in under a minute once cool, and the pieces stack flat. Wrap the sooty pieces in an old towel or a canvas bag to keep the car clean.

Keep food cold

  • Pack the cooler in layers: ice on the bottom, raw meat sealed in containers next, then ready-to-eat foods on top.
  • Use a separate cooler for drinks, since it gets opened constantly.
  • Keep coolers in the shade.
  • Discard perishable food that has sat out longer than 2 hours, or 1 hour on a day above 90°F.

A simple park menu for four

  1. Chicken skewers marinated at home in a lemon-garlic marinade.
  2. Corn on the cob, husked at home and wrapped in a towel.
  3. A sturdy grain salad that holds well in a cooler.
  4. Watermelon wedges.
  5. Grilled pound cake with berries over the last of the coals.

Leave no trace

Pack out all trash, including bottle caps and foil. Check under your blanket for scraps. Leave your spot cleaner than you found it.

Quick questions

Are personal grills allowed in parks?

It depends on the park. Check rules about personal grills, charcoal, burn bans and coal disposal before you go.

How do I dispose of charcoal at the beach?

Never bury it. Douse thoroughly and use a hot coal bin, or pack it out in a metal container.

How do I keep picnic food safe?

Keep raw meat iced in its own cooler, use separate utensils, cook to safe temps, and follow the two-hour rule.
