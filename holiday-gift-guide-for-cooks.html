Holiday Gift Guide for Cooks and Grillers

From a showstopper grill under the tree to stocking stuffers that cost less than lunch, a season's worth of gifts for everyone who loves to cook.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

The holidays are the season of cooking: roasts, cookies, big family breakfasts, and the quiet hope of warm weekends for grilling. That makes cooks some of the easiest people to shop for, as long as you choose wisely. This guide is organized by budget and by the kind of cook on your list, with ideas for wrapping, timing and making the gift feel personal.

Shopping principles

  • Choose quality over quantity. One durable tool beats five flimsy ones.
  • Think about storage. Many cooks are short on space. Compact or flat-packing gifts are welcome.
  • Give them something new to try. A new cooking method is exciting.
  • Order early. Holiday shipping is busy everywhere.

Holiday cooking on the grill

Gifting a grill in winter? Here are a few cold-weather cooks to get them started:

  • Spatchcocked chicken for a holiday dinner.
  • Reverse-seared prime rib slices or a thick ribeye.
  • Grilled sausages with mustard and sauerkraut.
  • Skillet chili and cornbread cooked over coals.
  • Grilled oysters with garlic butter.
  • Grilled pound cake with berries.

Cold-weather grilling tips

  • Charcoal burns faster in cold, windy weather. Use more fuel.
  • Allow extra time for preheating.
  • Wear insulated gloves and dress warmly.
  • Keep the grill out of the wind, but always grill outdoors in open air, never in a garage or enclosed space.
  • Use a headlamp or outdoor lighting.
  • Check temperatures with a thermometer; food cooks more slowly in cold weather.

The takeaway

The best holiday gifts for cooks are durable, useful, and invite them to try something new. A carbon steel grill, a griddle upgrade or a thermometer will be used for years. Add a recipe card, a promise of a cookout or a homemade spice rub, and the gift becomes a memory.

Going further: a December cook-along for the new grill

Gift a grill in December and the box might sit in the garage until spring. Give the recipient a reason to light it right away with a short, cold-weather cook-along you can do together.

Week one: the first fire

Assemble the grill together. A tab-and-slot design goes together by hand in under a minute, which makes a good little moment on its own. Light a half chimney of charcoal, spread the coals, and grill sausages and thick slices of bread. The goal is simply to learn how the fire behaves.

Week two: the season-builder

Cook something fatty that adds seasoning to new steel: bacon on a griddle plate, or burgers on the grates. Wipe the steel with a thin film of oil afterward while it is still warm.

Week three: the holiday roast slices

Reverse-sear a thick ribeye or strip steak over a two-zone fire. Use the thermometer to track the internal temperature on the cool side, then sear over the coals. Slice and serve with horseradish cream.

Week four: dessert by the fire

Bake a skillet cobbler or skillet cookie in a cast-iron pan set over a modest bed of coals, checking often. Serve with ice cream.

Gift tags for the cook-along

Write each week's menu on a tag and attach it to the package. The recipient opens a new tag each weekend in December.

Last-minute gifting

If you are too late for shipping, print a photo of the gift and a handwritten note announcing that it is on its way. Wrap the note with a jar of homemade rub or a bag of charcoal so there is something to open on the day.

Safety reminders for winter cooks

  • Charcoal produces carbon monoxide. Never use it indoors, in a garage, on an enclosed porch or in a tent.
  • Clear snow and ice from the cooking area for stable footing.
  • Keep the grill on level ground that cannot catch fire.
  • Keep a bucket of water or sand nearby.
