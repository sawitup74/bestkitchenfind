The Full Diner Breakfast on a Griddle

Bacon, hash browns, eggs any style and fluffy pancakes, all on one hot steel surface, timed so everything lands on the plate together.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

There is a reason every great diner cooks on a flat-top. A big sheet of hot steel lets one cook make bacon, potatoes, eggs and pancakes at the same time, using different heat zones on one surface. With a steel griddle over a charcoal fire, you can run the same play in your own backyard, campsite, or driveway. This is the guide to timing, heat management, and the little tricks that make a griddle breakfast feel like a professional short-order cook made it.

Why a griddle beats a stack of pans

A frying pan is a small, isolated pool of heat. Cook eggs in one, bacon in another and pancakes in a third, and you spend breakfast juggling handles and burners. A griddle is a single surface with multiple temperatures. The area right over the densest coals is hot enough to sear; the edges are gentle enough to keep things warm. Everything is in reach of one spatula.

Thick carbon steel is ideal. It holds a lot of heat, so cold food does not crash the temperature, and it seasons into a slick, naturally non-stick surface over time.

Setting up heat zones

Breakfast needs three zones:

  1. High heat for hash browns and searing.
  2. Medium heat for bacon, pancakes and eggs.
  3. Warm for holding finished food.

To build them, pour lit charcoal so the pile is deepest under one end of the griddle and thins toward the other end. On a Slot Grill with two Griddle add-on plates, the surface spans the whole cooking area, and you can bank coals to make the zones. With one griddle plate and one grate half, you can use the grate side for toast and the flat side for everything else.

Give the griddle 10 to 15 minutes to heat through. Test zones by flicking water: on high it vanishes instantly, on medium it dances for a couple of seconds, and on warm it sizzles slowly.

The menu and shopping list (for 4)

  • 1 pound thick-cut bacon
  • 1.5 pounds russet potatoes or a bag of frozen shredded hash browns
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 8 large eggs
  • Pancake batter (recipe below)
  • Butter, salt, pepper
  • Maple syrup, hot sauce, ketchup
  • Bread for toast

Make-ahead hash browns

The best hash browns start the night before. Grate the potatoes on the large holes of a box grater, rinse them in cold water until the water runs clear, then squeeze them as dry as possible in a clean kitchen towel. Spread on a sheet pan and refrigerate, uncovered or lightly covered, overnight. Dry potato shreds crisp much better.

Shortcut: frozen shredded hash browns are excellent. Thaw them and squeeze out the water before cooking.

Pancake batter

  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 2 cups buttermilk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 4 tablespoons melted butter
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla

Whisk the dry ingredients in one bowl and the wet in another. Stir them together until just combined. Lumps are fine; overmixing makes tough pancakes. Let the batter rest for 10 minutes while the griddle heats, or make it the night before and refrigerate. Pour it into a squeeze bottle or pitcher for easy pouring.

The timeline

Here is the order that gets everything done at once.

Minute 0: Bacon. Lay the strips on the medium zone. Bacon takes the longest and its fat will help everything else. Cook 8 to 12 minutes, flipping occasionally, until crisp. As it finishes, move it to the warm zone or a paper towel-lined plate.

Minute 2: Hash browns. Push a little bacon grease onto the high-heat zone, add a tablespoon of butter, then spread the potatoes in a thin, even layer, about half an inch thick. Season with salt and pepper and scatter diced onion on top. Do not touch them for 6 to 8 minutes. When the bottom is deep golden, flip in sections with a wide spatula, add another pat of butter, and cook 5 more minutes.

Minute 10: Pancakes. Wipe a clean spot on the medium zone and add a little butter. Pour 1/4 cup of batter per pancake. Flip when bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set, about 2 minutes, then cook another 1 to 2 minutes. Move to the warm zone.

Minute 15: Eggs. Eggs go last because they cook fast and are best fresh. Add butter to a medium spot and crack the eggs right onto the steel. See below for every style.

Minute 18: Toast. Butter bread and toast it directly on the griddle or on the grate.

Eggs every way on a griddle

  • Sunny side up: crack onto medium-low heat, cover loosely with a metal bowl or lid for a minute to set the whites without flipping.
  • Over easy: cook until the whites are set, then flip carefully and cook 15 to 20 seconds more.
  • Over medium: same as over easy, but cook 45 to 60 seconds after flipping.
  • Over hard: break the yolk with your spatula after flipping and cook until firm.
  • Scrambled: whisk eggs with a pinch of salt and a splash of milk, pour onto medium heat in a buttered spot, and push them gently with a spatula into soft curds. Pull them a little before they look done.
  • Egg rings: for neat, round eggs for breakfast sandwiches, use metal egg rings brushed with oil.

USDA advises cooking eggs until the whites and yolks are firm; runny yolks are a personal choice. Use fresh, refrigerated eggs.

Extras that level it up

  • French toast: dip thick slices of day-old brioche or challah in a custard of eggs, milk, cinnamon, vanilla and a pinch of salt. Griddle in butter over medium heat until golden.
  • Breakfast sausage patties: form ground pork seasoned with sage, black pepper, salt, red pepper flakes and a touch of maple into thin patties. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side.
  • Griddled tomatoes and mushrooms: halve tomatoes and cook them cut-side down with sliced mushrooms in butter for a British-style plate.
  • Breakfast sandwiches: stack an egg, cheese and sausage or bacon on a toasted English muffin.

Pancake perfection tips

The first pancake is a test. Use it to check the heat. If it browns too fast, the zone is too hot; move to a cooler spot.

Butter in small amounts. Too much butter makes pancakes greasy and spotty. Wipe on a thin layer with a folded paper towel.

Add mix-ins after pouring. Drop blueberries, chocolate chips or banana slices onto the batter right after it hits the griddle instead of stirring them in, so they distribute evenly and do not stain the batter.

Keep them warm. Hold finished pancakes at the warm edge of the griddle or in a low oven at 200°F, stacked loosely.

Breakfast for a crowd

For eight to ten people, scale up and assign roles. One person runs the bacon and potatoes; another handles pancakes and eggs. Keep finished items in foil pans on the cool side of the grill or a warm oven. Make pancake batter and hash brown prep the night before. Set out a coffee station and toppings so people serve themselves.

Cleaning the griddle after breakfast

While still hot, scrape off grease and food bits with a flat metal spatula. Pour a small splash of water on stubborn spots and scrape as it steams. Wipe dry with paper towels. Then rub on a very thin coat of oil or seasoning balm while the steel is warm, to protect it and build seasoning. Never let carbon steel sit wet.

Troubleshooting

Hash browns are soggy. The potatoes were wet, the layer too thick, or you flipped too soon. Squeeze them dry and leave them alone.

Pancakes are raw inside. The heat was too high. Cook on medium.

Eggs stick. The steel was not seasoned well or there was not enough butter. Eggs are the hardest test for any griddle; seasoning improves with every cook.

Bacon curls. Cook on medium, not high, and press it down with a spatula, or use a bacon press.

The takeaway

A griddle breakfast is about rhythm, not recipes. Start the slowest food first, use your heat zones, keep a warm area for finished items and cook eggs last. Follow that pattern and you will serve a full diner breakfast that arrives hot, all at once, with only one surface to clean.

Quick questions

What order should I cook a griddle breakfast in?

Bacon first, then hash browns, pancakes, eggs and toast last, so everything finishes together.

How do I make crispy hash browns?

Rinse and squeeze grated potatoes very dry, spread thin over high heat, and do not flip until the bottom is deep golden.

Can a charcoal-fired griddle make pancakes?

Yes. Use the medium-heat zone and test with one pancake to dial in the temperature.
