Compound Butters, Chimichurri and Finishing Sauces

Six make-ahead flavor bombs that turn a simple grilled steak, fish or vegetable into a restaurant plate in seconds.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Some of the best flavor in grilled food comes at the very end. A pat of herb butter melting on a hot steak, a spoonful of bright green chimichurri over grilled chicken, or a drizzle of garlic-chili oil on vegetables can transform simple food. Finishing sauces are easy, make ahead well and are the secret to restaurant-style plates.

Why finish with a sauce

Grilled food has smoke, char and caramelization. A finishing sauce adds contrast: fresh herbs, acid, richness or heat. Because finishing sauces are added after cooking, they do not burn and their fresh flavors stay bright.

Compound butters

A compound butter is softened butter mixed with flavorings, rolled into a log and chilled. Slice off a coin and set it on hot food.

How to make compound butter

  1. Soften 1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter at room temperature.
  2. Mix in flavorings with a fork.
  3. Spoon onto plastic wrap or parchment, roll into a log, and twist the ends.
  4. Refrigerate up to 1 week or freeze up to 3 months.

Butter 1: Garlic-herb butter

  • 1/2 cup softened butter
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely grated
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 1 tablespoon chopped chives
  • 1 teaspoon chopped thyme
  • 1/2 teaspoon flaky salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

Great on steak, corn, bread, potatoes and fish.

Butter 2: Blue cheese butter

  • 1/2 cup softened butter
  • 1/3 cup crumbled blue cheese
  • 1 tablespoon chopped chives
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Perfect on steak and burgers.

Butter 3: Chili-lime butter

  • 1/2 cup softened butter
  • Zest of 1 lime and 1 tablespoon juice
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
  • 1 tablespoon chopped cilantro
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Great on corn, shrimp, fish and chicken.

Butter 4: Miso-honey butter

  • 1/2 cup softened butter
  • 2 tablespoons white miso
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 scallion, finely chopped

Wonderful on salmon, corn, mushrooms and pork.

Chimichurri

Chimichurri is a bright, herby, garlicky sauce from Argentina and Uruguay, traditionally served with grilled meat.

Classic chimichurri

  • 1 cup finely chopped flat-leaf parsley
  • 2 tablespoons fresh oregano leaves, chopped (or 2 teaspoons dried)
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1/4 cup red wine vinegar
  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Mix the garlic, vinegar, salt and pepper flakes and let them sit for 10 minutes to mellow the garlic. Stir in the herbs and oil. Rest for 20 minutes before using. Best the same day, but keeps for 2 to 3 days refrigerated.

Tips

  • Chop by hand for the best texture. A food processor can make it pasty.
  • Use plenty of salt and acid.
  • Let it sit for flavors to combine.

Uses

  • Steak, especially skirt and flank
  • Grilled chicken
  • Sausages
  • Grilled vegetables
  • Fried eggs
  • Potatoes

Other finishing sauces

Salsa verde

An Italian-style green sauce: parsley, basil, capers, anchovy, garlic, lemon zest, red wine vinegar and olive oil. Great on fish, lamb and vegetables.

Gremolata

A dry mix of lemon zest, garlic and parsley. Sprinkle on grilled fish, chicken and vegetables.

Garlic-chili oil

Gently heat 1/2 cup olive oil with 4 sliced garlic cloves and 1 teaspoon chili flakes until the garlic is just golden. Cool. Drizzle over vegetables, fish or pizza.

Herb yogurt sauce

Mix 1 cup Greek yogurt, 1 grated garlic clove, 2 tablespoons chopped dill or mint, lemon juice and salt. Serve with grilled lamb, chicken and vegetables.

Romesco

A smoky Spanish sauce of roasted red peppers, toasted almonds, garlic, sherry vinegar, olive oil and smoked paprika. Blend smooth. Excellent with grilled vegetables, fish and chicken.

The takeaway

Finishing sauces are the fastest way to elevate grilled food. A coin of compound butter, a spoonful of chimichurri or a drizzle of garlic-chili oil adds freshness, richness and contrast. Make them ahead, keep them on hand, and every grilled meal will taste like it came from a restaurant.

Going further: technique notes for finishing sauces

Getting butter to the right softness

Butter for compound butter should be soft enough to press a finger into easily but not shiny or melting. Cut a cold stick into small cubes and leave it on the counter for 30 to 45 minutes. If you are in a hurry, grate cold butter on the large holes of a box grater; the shreds soften in minutes. Never microwave it to soften, because partially melted butter will not hold its shape once chilled and the flavorings sink.

Rolling a neat log

Spoon the butter in a rough line along the long edge of parchment. Fold the paper over, then press a ruler or bench scraper against the fold while pulling the bottom sheet toward you. The butter squeezes into a tight, even cylinder. Twist the ends like a candy wrapper and chill. Cut coins with a warm knife for clean edges.

Timing the melt

Set butter on meat during the rest, not on the grill. The heat of the resting steak melts the butter into a glossy sauce that mixes with the juices on the board. Fish and vegetables need the butter right as they come off, since they cool faster.

Keeping herbs green

Bright herb sauces darken over time as the leaves oxidize. To keep chimichurri and salsa verde vivid:

  • Chop with a very sharp knife, which bruises leaves less.
  • Add the oil soon after chopping to coat the herbs.
  • Store with plastic wrap pressed on the surface.
  • Add acid close to serving if you are making the sauce far ahead.

Taming raw garlic

Raw garlic grows harsher as it sits in oil. Soaking minced garlic in the vinegar for ten minutes before adding the oil, as in the chimichurri recipe, softens its bite. For mild sauces, use half the garlic or blanch the cloves in boiling water for 30 seconds first.

Food safety for garlic and herb oils

Garlic and fresh herbs stored in oil at room temperature can create a risk of botulism. Keep homemade garlic oils and herb sauces in the refrigerator and use them within a few days. Do not leave them sitting at room temperature for long periods, and do not give homemade garlic-in-oil as a shelf-stable gift.

Using the griddle for sauces

A flat-top is perfect for warming finishing sauces. Push a pat of compound butter onto the hot plate, add sliced mushrooms or shrimp, and toss until coated and glossy. Or melt garlic-herb butter on the griddle and press halved rolls into it for toasted, buttery buns in a minute.

Make a freezer sampler

Make four butters on a rainy afternoon, roll each into a log, wrap and label them, and freeze. With a sampler in the freezer, a plain Tuesday steak can get blue cheese butter, a salmon fillet can get miso-honey and grilled corn can get chili-lime with no effort on cook night. Slice frozen logs with a knife dipped in hot water.

Plate like a restaurant

Slice the protein, fan it on a warm board, spoon the sauce in a line across the slices rather than drowning them, and finish with flaky salt and a few whole herb leaves. It takes ten seconds and looks like a restaurant plate.

Quick questions

How long does compound butter last?

About a week in the refrigerator or 3 months in the freezer.

How long does chimichurri keep?

It is best the same day and keeps 2 to 3 days refrigerated.

Can I make chimichurri in a food processor?

Yes, but pulse briefly; hand chopping gives better texture.

Is garlic in oil safe to keep at room temperature?

No. Refrigerate homemade garlic and herb oils and use them within a few days.
