The Tailgate Grilling Playbook

Packing lists, parking-lot menus, setup, safety and cleanup for a game-day cookout that beats anything in the stadium.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Tailgating is a celebration of food, friends and team spirit, and the grill is its heart. A great tailgate starts early, smells incredible and keeps everyone fed until kickoff. It also requires planning: you have limited space, no kitchen and often strict rules. This playbook covers everything from packing to cleanup.

Know the rules first

Every stadium, campus and venue has its own tailgating rules. Before you go, check:

  • Hours: when lots open and when tailgating must end.
  • Grill restrictions: some venues ban open flames or charcoal, or require them a certain distance from vehicles.
  • Charcoal disposal: many lots provide hot coal bins.
  • Alcohol policies.
  • Space limits: some lots allow only one parking space per vehicle.
  • Tent and table rules.

Choosing the right grill

A tailgate grill should be:

  • Portable: easy to carry from the car.
  • Quick to set up: you want to cook, not assemble.
  • Sturdy: able to handle a crowd of food and heavy pans.
  • Stable: safe on tailgates, tables or pavement.

Flat-pack steel grills are ideal. Broken down, a Compact is roughly an inch deep and around 14 pounds; a Full Size is roughly an inch and a quarter and around 27.6 pounds. Both slide into a trunk or truck bed alongside coolers and chairs, go together by hand in well under a couple of minutes without a single tool, and lift the coals off the ground (2⅞ inches of clearance for the Compact and 3⅝ for the Full Size), which keeps much of the heat away from whatever is underneath. Even so, keep any hot grill away from vehicles, fabric and fuel.

For bigger groups, the Full Size's 231 square inches of cooking area fits a lot of brats and burgers. For smaller groups, the Compact's 146 square inches is plenty.

A make-ahead tailgate menu

Do almost all the prep at home. The lot is for cooking and celebrating.

Bratwurst with beer and onions

Simmer brats with sliced onions in beer at home, or in a foil pan on the grill. Then grill them over direct heat to char. Serve in buns with the onions and mustard.

Smash burgers or sliders

Portion beef into balls at home and pack them between parchment in a container. Bring a griddle plate and smash them on site. Sliders on small buns stretch further for a crowd.

Chicken wings

Season wings at home and pack them in a zip-top bag. Grill over indirect heat until cooked through, about 30 to 40 minutes, then crisp over direct heat and toss in buffalo or barbecue sauce.

Grilled street corn

Grill husked corn, then brush with mayo, sprinkle with cotija cheese, chili powder and lime juice.

Pulled pork sliders

Smoke or slow-cook a pork shoulder at home the day before. Shred it and pack it with sauce. Reheat in a foil pan on the cool side of the grill.

Breakfast tailgate

For early kickoffs, a griddle breakfast is unbeatable: bacon, breakfast tacos, pancakes and coffee. Prep breakfast taco fillings and pancake batter at home.

Lot setup

  1. Park, then set up the canopy and table first.
  2. Place the grill away from the car, the canopy and foot traffic, on a stable surface.
  3. Light the charcoal in a chimney.
  4. Set up the food table and coolers in the shade.
  5. Start grilling as soon as the coals are ready.

Food safety on the road

  • Keep raw meat in a separate cooler with plenty of ice, at or below 40°F.
  • Pack meat frozen if you have a long drive; it helps keep the cooler cold.
  • Use separate tongs and plates for raw and cooked food.
  • Check temperatures: burgers and sausages 160°F, chicken 165°F.
  • Do not leave perishable food out more than two hours, or one hour above 90°F.

Cleanup and coal disposal

Coal disposal is one of the most important parts of tailgating.

  • Use a designated hot coal bin if your venue has one.
  • Never dump hot coals on the pavement, grass or into trash cans.
  • If no bin is available, douse the coals thoroughly with water, stir, and douse again. Then transfer to a metal container to take home.
  • Let the grill cool completely before packing. Thick steel holds heat longer than you expect, so allow plenty of time, and pack it in a heat-safe bag or metal container if needed.

The takeaway

Plan ahead, pack smart, follow the rules and do as much prep as possible at home. A portable, sturdy grill and a good menu will make your tailgate the one everyone wants to visit.

Going further: the pro tailgater's checklist

Pack the night before

Lay out everything the night before and check it against a list. The most commonly forgotten items: lighter or matches, tongs, a trash bag, hand wipes, salt and the bottle opener. Store your tailgate kit in a single bin so it is ready every game.

The tailgate bin

  • Flat-pack grill and its carry bag
  • Chimney starter and a lighter
  • Charcoal in a sealed bin to keep the car clean
  • Tongs, spatula, and a thermometer
  • Heat-resistant gloves
  • Cutting board and knife in a sheath
  • Paper towels, wipes, trash bags
  • A metal can with a lid for cooled ash
  • A small folding table

Stadium rules

Many stadiums and lots have rules about grills and charcoal. Some require coals to be dumped in designated bins, some ban open flames near vehicles, and some limit the size of setups. Check the venue's tailgate policy before the season starts.

Car safety

  • Grill away from vehicles, tents and crowds.
  • Never grill under a canopy or awning.
  • Keep a bucket of water or a fire extinguisher handy.
  • Do not pack the grill until it is fully cool.

A make-ahead strategy

The best tailgates are mostly prepped at home:

  • Marinate meat and pack it in sealed containers.
  • Form burger patties and stack them between parchment.
  • Skewer kebabs at home.
  • Chop toppings and pack them in containers.
  • Make sauces and sides ahead.

At the lot, you only need to light the fire and cook.

Cold-weather tailgates

Late-season games can be frigid. Bring extra charcoal, since the fire burns faster in cold wind. Keep food covered, warm buns on the grill, and serve chili from a thermos alongside hot sausages.

Clean up and go

Douse coals, let the steel cool, scrape the grates, and stack the pieces flat. With a flat-pack grill, cleanup takes minutes, and you can be in your seat before kickoff.

Quick questions

What grill is best for tailgating?

A portable, sturdy grill that sets up quickly. Flat-pack steel grills fit easily in a trunk or truck bed.

How do I dispose of charcoal at a tailgate?

Use the venue's hot coal bin if provided. Otherwise douse thoroughly with water and take the ash home in a metal container.

How do I keep tailgate food safe?

Keep raw meat in its own iced cooler, use separate utensils, and cook to safe temperatures.
