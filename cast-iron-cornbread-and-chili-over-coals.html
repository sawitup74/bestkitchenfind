Skillet Chili and Cornbread Over Coals

A smoky pot of beef and bean chili and a crackling skillet of cornbread, both cooked over live charcoal on a sturdy grill frame.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Some meals are about speed. This one is about the pleasure of slowing down. A pot of chili bubbling over charcoal for an hour picks up a subtle smokiness that you simply cannot get on a stovetop, and cornbread baked in a cast-iron skillet over coals develops a dark, crunchy crust that steals the show. It is the kind of dinner that fills a backyard with good smells on a cool evening and keeps everyone lingering around the fire.

Why cook chili and cornbread on a grill

Charcoal adds three things a stove cannot: gentle smoke, radiant heat around the sides of the pot, and the sheer fun of cooking outside. A Dutch oven or heavy cast-iron skillet sitting on a grill grate over coals cooks much like it would on a camp stove, but with that bonus layer of flavor.

To do it safely, you need a grill frame strong enough to support heavy cast iron. A full Dutch oven of chili can weigh 12 pounds or more. Thick steel grills handle this easily; the Slot Grill Compact and Full Size are both built strong enough to carry cast iron without flexing, which is exactly what you want when a pot of boiling chili is involved.

  • A cast-iron skillet, 10 or 12 inches across, for the cornbread

Fire management for long cooks

Simmering a pot of chili for an hour requires steady, moderate heat, not a raging fire.

  1. Light a full chimney of charcoal.
  2. When the coals are ashed over, pour them into the grill in an even layer.
  3. Set the Dutch oven on the grate over the coals.
  4. Every 30 to 40 minutes, add 8 to 12 fresh briquettes or a handful of lump charcoal to keep the heat going. Add them to the edges so they catch from the existing coals.

Briquettes are a good choice here because they burn longer and more steadily than lump charcoal.

Smoky beef and bean chili

Serves 6 to 8

  • 2 pounds ground beef (80/20), or 1 pound ground beef and 1 pound cubed chuck
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 1 jalapeño, minced
  • 5 cloves garlic, minced
  • 3 tablespoons chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon cayenne, more or less to taste
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 (28-ounce) can crushed tomatoes
  • 2 (15-ounce) cans kidney or pinto beans, drained
  • 1 cup beef stock or a bottle of beer
  • 1 tablespoon cocoa powder
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Set the Dutch oven over the coals and let it heat for 5 minutes.
  2. Add a splash of oil and the beef. Break it up and brown it well, about 8 to 10 minutes. If using cubed chuck, brown it first in batches, then add the ground beef. Spoon off excess fat, leaving about 2 tablespoons.
  3. Add the onion, bell pepper and jalapeño. Cook until softened, about 5 minutes.
  4. Add the garlic, chili powder, cumin, paprika, oregano, cinnamon and cayenne. Stir for 1 minute until fragrant.
  5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for another minute.
  6. Add the tomatoes, beans, stock or beer, cocoa and brown sugar, then salt and pepper to taste.
  7. Bring to a simmer. Slide the pot to a slightly cooler part of the grill or let the coals burn down a bit so it simmers gently.
  8. Simmer uncovered or partly covered for 1 to 1.5 hours, stirring every 10 to 15 minutes, until thick. If using cubed chuck, simmer until tender, about 2 hours.
  9. Stir in the vinegar and adjust the seasoning.

Toppings bar

  • Shredded cheddar or pepper jack
  • Sour cream or Greek yogurt
  • Sliced scallions and diced red onion
  • Pickled jalapeños
  • Chopped cilantro
  • Crushed tortilla chips
  • Avocado
  • Lime wedges

Cast-iron cornbread

The crackly crust of skillet cornbread comes from pouring batter into a scorching-hot, well-greased skillet.

Serves 8

  • 1.5 cups yellow cornmeal (medium grind)
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour
  • 2 tablespoons sugar (optional, but traditional in many households)
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1.5 cups buttermilk
  • 2 large eggs
  • 4 tablespoons melted butter, plus 2 tablespoons for the skillet

Method

  1. Whisk together the cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda and salt.
  2. In a separate bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs and melted butter.
  3. Combine wet and dry ingredients, stirring until just mixed. Let rest 10 minutes.
  4. Put the empty skillet over direct coals with 2 tablespoons of butter or bacon fat. Heat until the fat is shimmering and starting to brown.
  5. Pour the batter into the hot skillet. It should sizzle loudly.
  6. Move the skillet to the coal-free half of a two-zone fire.

Baking over coals: the tricky part

A skillet sitting directly over coals cooks from the bottom, which can burn the base before the top sets. Two methods solve this:

Indirect method: After pouring the batter, move the skillet to the cool side of the grill and cover it with a sheet of foil or an inverted metal pan to trap heat. Bake for 25 to 35 minutes, rotating every 10 minutes, until a toothpick comes out clean.

Coals-on-lid method: If you have a Dutch oven with a flanged lid, place the batter inside the Dutch oven, set it over a few coals and pile coals on the lid. This bakes from top and bottom, like an oven.

Expect your first few attempts to come out a little darker on the bottom. That crunchy bottom is the best part.

Variations

Cheddar-jalapeño cornbread: fold 1 cup shredded sharp cheddar and 2 minced jalapeños into the batter.

Honey butter cornbread: brush the top with melted butter and honey right out of the skillet.

Bacon cornbread: cook chopped bacon in the skillet, use the fat to grease it and fold the bacon into the batter.

White bean chicken chili: swap the beef for shredded grilled chicken, use white beans, green chilies, cumin, chicken stock and a little cream cheese.

Vegetarian chili: use three types of beans, sweet potatoes, corn, zucchini and a bit of chipotle in adobo.

Make it a night

Chili and cornbread make perfect cool-weather cookout food. Light the fire in the late afternoon, get the chili going and invite friends over. While the chili simmers, everyone can gather, snack and enjoy the smell. Bake the cornbread about 45 minutes before serving.

Cast-iron care after cooking

Clean the skillet and Dutch oven while they are still warm. Scrape out food, rinse with hot water and scrub with a brush. Dry thoroughly over the coals, then wipe on a thin coat of oil. Acidic tomato-based chili can slightly strip seasoning, so a quick re-oil is a good habit.

The takeaway

There are few things more satisfying than a pot of chili bubbling over a charcoal fire, paired with a golden skillet of cornbread. Take your time, enjoy the process and invite people over. This is cookout food for every season.

Cook's notebook

Make it a one-fire supper

Start the chili first over a full bed of coals in a Dutch oven or deep skillet, since it benefits from a long, gentle simmer. While it bubbles, mix the cornbread batter. When the chili is nearly done, move it to the cooler side, rake fresh coals under the other side, and bake the cornbread in a preheated, buttered skillet. Check the cornbread often by sight and with a toothpick; open-fire baking runs hotter at the bottom than an oven, so a ring of coals around the edge rather than directly under the center prevents scorching. Serve the chili ladled over wedges of warm cornbread, with shredded cheese, sliced scallions and sour cream on the side. One fire, two cast-iron pans and a hearty meal for a crowd on a cool evening.

Quick questions

Can I put a Dutch oven on a charcoal grill?

Yes, as long as the grill frame is strong enough to support heavy cast iron. Thick steel grills handle it easily.

How do I keep cornbread from burning on the bottom over coals?

Preheat the greased skillet, pour the batter, then move it to indirect heat and cover it to trap heat.

How long should chili simmer over charcoal?

One to one and a half hours for ground beef, about two hours if using cubed chuck.
