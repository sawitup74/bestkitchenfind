Hibachi-Style Fried Rice and Garlic Butter Shrimp

The teppanyaki-house experience at home: smoky fried rice, sweet onions, zucchini and shrimp seared on hot steel with a garlic-soy butter.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Few restaurant meals are as fun as a teppanyaki grill house, where a chef flips shrimp tails into his hat and builds a volcano out of onions. You probably will not set any onions on fire in your backyard, but you can absolutely recreate the flavors: the garlicky fried rice, the sweet caramelized vegetables, the buttery seared shrimp and steak, and the creamy, tangy dipping sauce. A big steel griddle over charcoal is the perfect tool, and the open air means you do not fill your kitchen with smoke.

The secret ingredient: garlic butter

Hibachi cooking relies heavily on butter flavored with garlic and soy. Make a batch first:

  • 1/2 cup (1 stick) softened butter
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon chopped parsley (optional)

Mash together and refrigerate. You will use it on everything.

The essential rule: cold rice

Fried rice made with freshly cooked rice turns mushy and sticky because the grains are still full of moisture. Use rice cooked at least a day ahead and refrigerated uncovered or loosely covered so it dries out. The grains separate and fry into distinct, slightly crisp morsels.

Medium-grain or short-grain white rice is typical of teppanyaki restaurants. Jasmine rice also works well. For four people, cook 2 cups of dry rice to get about 6 cups cooked.

Shortcut: if you forgot to make rice ahead, spread freshly cooked rice on a sheet pan and refrigerate or freeze for 30 minutes to dry it.

Ingredients (for 4)

Fried rice:

  • 6 cups cold cooked rice
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 cup diced onion
  • 1 cup frozen peas and diced carrots, thawed
  • 3 tablespoons garlic butter
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 3 scallions, sliced
  • Salt and pepper

Vegetables:

  • 2 zucchini, cut into half-moons
  • 1 large onion, cut into thick wedges
  • 8 ounces mushrooms, sliced
  • Garlic butter and soy sauce

Proteins:

  • 1 pound large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 pound sirloin or ribeye, cut into 1-inch cubes, or boneless chicken thighs, diced
  • Garlic butter, soy sauce, salt and pepper

The sauces

Yum yum sauce (the pink one)

  • 1 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons melted butter
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste or ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon paprika
  • 2 tablespoons water to thin

Whisk until smooth, then chill for at least an hour so the flavors meld.

Ginger sauce

Blend 1/2 cup soy sauce, 1/4 cup rice vinegar, a 2-inch piece of fresh ginger, 1/2 small onion, 1 garlic clove, 1 tablespoon sugar and 1 tablespoon lemon juice until smooth.

Setting up the griddle

You need a big, hot surface. A full griddle setup on a larger grill gives you room to keep rice, vegetables and protein moving at the same time. On a Slot Grill Full Size with two Griddle add-on plates, the entire 20.5 × 11.25 in. grate becomes flat steel. On a Compact, cook in stages, starting with vegetables and protein, then pushing them aside to keep warm while you fry the rice.

Fire up a full chimney, then pour and rake the coals in an even layer under the griddle, a little deeper under one end for a high-heat zone. Heat for 10 to 15 minutes until very hot.

Cooking order

1. Vegetables

Add a tablespoon of oil and a tablespoon of garlic butter to the medium-high zone. Add the onion wedges and zucchini and cook, tossing with two spatulas, for 4 to 5 minutes. Add mushrooms and another knob of garlic butter, splash with soy sauce and cook until the vegetables are tender and caramelized at the edges, 3 to 4 minutes more. Push them to the cooler side.

2. Steak or chicken

Season the cubes with salt and pepper. Add oil to the hottest zone and lay the meat in a single layer. Sear without moving for 1 to 2 minutes, then toss. Add garlic butter and a splash of soy. Steak cubes take 3 to 5 minutes for medium-rare; chicken takes 6 to 8 minutes and should reach 165°F. Push to the warm zone.

3. Shrimp

Add the shrimp to the hot zone with garlic butter. Cook 1 to 2 minutes per side until pink and opaque. Squeeze a little lemon on top. Move aside.

4. Fried rice

Scrape the hot zone clean. Add a tablespoon of oil and the onions, and cook for 2 minutes. Add the peas and carrots and toss. Add the rice and spread it out over the griddle, breaking up any clumps with your spatula. Let it sit for a minute so some grains get crispy.

Add the garlic butter and toss. Push the rice to one side, crack the eggs into the open space and scramble them quickly. When they are mostly set, chop them into small pieces with the spatula and mix them into the rice.

Drizzle with soy sauce and sesame oil, toss well, and season with salt and pepper. Fold in the scallions.

Serve it like a pro

Pile the fried rice on plates, then top with vegetables and proteins. Set out small bowls of yum yum sauce and ginger sauce for dipping. Garnish with toasted sesame seeds.

For a fun, interactive dinner, have everyone gather around the grill and serve straight off the griddle onto their plates as each item is ready, just like a teppanyaki show.

Adding the show

You do not have to stop at flavors. A few easy tricks bring some theater:

  • Toss shrimp tails with your spatula for a kid to catch in a cup.
  • Make an onion volcano: stack thick onion rings into a cone, pour a small splash of water in the center for a puff of steam (skip the alcohol in the restaurant version).
  • Use two spatulas and drum them on the steel between steps.
  • Slide finished food onto plates with a flourish.

Variations

  • Chicken fried rice: dice chicken thighs and cook them into the rice.
  • Kimchi fried rice: add chopped kimchi and a spoonful of gochujang, top with a fried egg.
  • Pineapple fried rice: add grilled pineapple chunks, cashews and a little curry powder.
  • Vegetable fried rice: double the vegetables and add tofu cubes seared until golden.
  • Scallops: sear large, dry sea scallops for 90 seconds per side over high heat with garlic butter.

Clean as you go

Scrape the griddle between each step to remove burned bits that would otherwise stick to the next item. Keep a squeeze bottle of water and a bench scraper handy.

When you finish, scrape the griddle clean while hot, wipe it with paper towels, and finish with a thin coat of oil. Soy sauce and sugar can leave sticky residue, so be thorough.

Troubleshooting

Fried rice is mushy. The rice was too fresh or too wet. Use day-old rice.

Rice is bland. Use more garlic butter and soy sauce, and season with salt. Restaurant fried rice is richly seasoned.

Rice sticks to the griddle. Add more oil, use a hotter zone and keep the rice moving after the initial crisping.

Steak is tough. Use a tender cut such as sirloin or ribeye and do not overcook it.

The takeaway

Hibachi night is equal parts dinner and entertainment. With cold rice, plenty of garlic butter and a hot steel griddle, you can bring the teppanyaki experience home. Gather everyone around the grill and enjoy the show.

Cook's notebook

Rice matters most

Fried rice is best made with cold, day-old rice. Fresh rice is too moist and turns mushy and sticky on the griddle. If you must use fresh rice, spread it on a sheet pan and refrigerate it uncovered for an hour or two to dry the surface. Break up clumps with your hands before it hits the steel. Spread it thin across the hot plate and let it sit undisturbed for a minute to toast, then toss. Season at the end with soy sauce around the edges, where it sizzles and caramelizes, rather than pouring it into the middle of the pile.

Quick questions

Why use day-old rice for fried rice?

Fresh rice is too moist and turns mushy. Chilled, dried rice separates and crisps on the griddle.

What is yum yum sauce?

A creamy, tangy dipping sauce made from mayonnaise, butter, tomato paste, vinegar, sugar and spices.

Can I make hibachi fried rice on a charcoal grill?

Yes, on a griddle plate over hot coals. The steel gets plenty hot and the smoke stays outdoors.
