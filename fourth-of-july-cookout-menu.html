A Fourth of July Cookout Menu That Feeds Everyone

A red, white and blue menu of burgers, ribs-style chicken, corn, slaws and a grilled berry dessert, with a timeline that lets you watch the fireworks.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

The Fourth of July is the biggest grilling day of the year in America, and for good reason: long summer evenings, family and friends, and food that tastes best cooked outdoors. A great Independence Day cookout should feel festive without being exhausting. This menu covers every course, from snacks to dessert, with a timeline built so the host gets to enjoy the party too.

Shopping list for 12 to 15 guests

  • 5 pounds ground chuck (80/20)
  • 6 pounds chicken drumsticks (about 24)
  • 2 pounds sausages
  • 3 bell peppers and 2 onions
  • 15 ears of corn
  • 1 large head of green cabbage, 1 small head of red cabbage, 2 carrots
  • 3 cans of beans plus bacon and onion
  • 6 tomatoes, 2 cucumbers, 1 red onion
  • 1 large watermelon
  • 1 dozen eggs
  • 2 pounds strawberries, 1 pint blueberries, 1 pint raspberries
  • 1 store-bought or homemade pound cake
  • 2 pints heavy cream
  • Buns, cheese, lettuce, tomatoes, onions, pickles, condiments

The recipes

All-American cheeseburgers

Divide the chuck into 6-ounce portions and gently form patties about 3/4 inch thick. Press a shallow dimple in the center of each so it does not puff up into a dome while cooking. Season generously with salt and pepper right before grilling.

Grill over direct, high heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side. Add cheese during the last minute and cover each burger with a metal bowl to melt it. Cook to 160°F. Toast buns on the grate for 30 seconds.

Sticky barbecue drumsticks

Season drumsticks with a rub of paprika, brown sugar, salt, onion powder, garlic powder, black pepper and a little cayenne. Grill over indirect heat for 35 to 45 minutes, turning every 10 minutes, until the meat reaches 175 to 185°F. Brush with barbecue sauce during the last 10 minutes and move over direct heat for a minute or two per side to caramelize. Drumsticks are inexpensive, kid-friendly and hold well.

Sausages with peppers and onions

Grill sausages over medium heat, turning often, until cooked through. Toss sliced peppers and onions with oil and salt and cook them in a foil pan or on a griddle until soft and charred.

Corn with herb butter

Mix softened butter with chopped chives, parsley, a little garlic and lemon zest. Grill husked corn over direct heat for 10 to 12 minutes, turning, until charred in spots. Brush with herb butter.

Creamy coleslaw

Shred cabbage and carrots. Whisk 1 cup mayonnaise, 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar, 1 tablespoon sugar, 1 teaspoon celery seed, salt and pepper. Toss with the vegetables an hour before serving.

Smoky baked beans

Cook 6 slices of chopped bacon and a diced onion in a cast-iron skillet or Dutch oven. Add 3 cans of drained beans, 1/2 cup barbecue sauce, 1/4 cup brown sugar, 2 tablespoons mustard and a splash of apple cider vinegar. Simmer on the cool side of the grill for 45 minutes.

Tomato and cucumber salad

Chop tomatoes and cucumbers, slice red onion thin. Dress with olive oil, red wine vinegar, salt, pepper and fresh basil.

Grilled pound cake with berries

Slice pound cake 1 inch thick. Brush lightly with melted butter. Grill over medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes per side until golden and marked. Top with sliced strawberries, blueberries, raspberries and whipped cream. Red, white and blue on a plate.

Fruit flag skewers

Thread strawberries, banana slices and blueberries onto skewers for kids. Arrange them on a platter in the shape of a flag.

The timeline

Two days before

  • Shop for everything except the most perishable produce.
  • Make the rub and herb butter.
  • Make the baked beans (they get better overnight).

The day before

  • Shop for produce.
  • Make deviled eggs and pimento cheese.
  • Shred cabbage for the slaw.
  • Form burger patties.
  • Season the drumsticks and refrigerate.
  • Make lemonade and iced tea.

Morning of

  • Make the coleslaw dressing.
  • Cut watermelon.
  • Chop salad vegetables.
  • Assemble fruit skewers.
  • Whip the cream and chill.

Three hours before

  • Set up tables, shade, coolers and seating.
  • Put drinks on ice.

Two hours before

  • Light the charcoal and build a two-zone fire.
  • Start the drumsticks on the cool side.

One hour before

  • Put out snacks as guests arrive.
  • Warm the baked beans on the cool side.
  • Grill corn.

Mealtime

  • Grill burgers and sausages in waves.
  • Toss the coleslaw and salad.

After dinner

  • As coals burn down, grill the pound cake for dessert.

Safety on the Fourth

  • Keep the grill away from fireworks, and fireworks away from the grill.
  • Set the grill up on a stable, non-flammable surface away from buildings and overhanging branches.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher or bucket of water nearby.
  • Keep perishable food cold, and follow the two-hour rule (one hour above 90°F).
  • Supervise children around the grill.

Make it your own

  • Regional twist: go Carolina-style with pulled pork and vinegar slaw, Texas-style with brisket sliders, or New England with grilled lobster tails.
  • Vegetarian guests: add grilled portobello burgers and halloumi skewers.
  • Gluten-free: offer lettuce wraps and gluten-free buns.

The takeaway

The Fourth of July cookout is about abundance and celebration. Plan a menu that mixes make-ahead dishes with grill favorites, prep as much as possible the day before, and cook in waves. Then sit back, enjoy the company, and watch the fireworks.

Going further: the holiday-day playbook

The day before

  • Make sauces, slaw dressing and compound butter.
  • Dry brine chicken.
  • Form burger patties and refrigerate between parchment.
  • Bake dessert or prep fruit.
  • Chill drinks.

The morning of

  • Set up tables, shade and seating.
  • Prep toppings and refrigerate.
  • Set up the grill in a safe, open spot away from the house.

Fireworks and fire

Holiday fireworks and charcoal fires do not mix. Keep fireworks well away from the grill and from the charcoal bag, and never let anyone handle fireworks near the cooking area. Follow your local laws on fireworks, and check for burn bans during dry summers.

Red, white and blue dessert on the grill

Grill thick slices of pound cake until marked, then top with strawberries, blueberries and whipped cream. Fast, festive and easy.

Keep it cool

July heat is tough on food. Follow the 1-hour rule for perishables above 90°F. Serve sides in bowls set over ice, and grill in batches so food is fresh.

Cook's notebook

Scaling the menu

Plan on about 1/3 pound of meat per adult when there are plenty of sides, and about 1/2 pound when meat is the star. Count on 1 1/2 burgers or hot dogs per guest, plus extra for teenagers. For sides, a half cup to a cup per person of each salad is plenty when you offer three or more. Buy a bag of ice per four to five guests on a hot day. Write the numbers down before you shop, and you will not run out of buns at the worst possible moment or end up with three pounds of leftover potato salad. When in doubt, extra hot dogs are the cheapest insurance in the cooler. Keep a second bag of charcoal on hand so a long, lazy afternoon of grilling never ends early.

Quick questions

How much burger meat do I need for 15 people?

About 5 pounds of ground chuck makes roughly 13 to 14 six-ounce patties; add sausages and chicken to round it out.

How do I keep burgers from puffing up?

Press a shallow dimple into the center of each patty before grilling.

Can I make baked beans ahead?

Yes, they taste better made a day or two ahead and reheated on the grill.
