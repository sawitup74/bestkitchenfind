Charred Shrimp Tacos with Lime Crema

Fast, smoky shrimp skewers, a crunchy cabbage slaw, and a five-minute crema. Taco night moves outside and finishes in under half an hour.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Shrimp might be the most underrated thing you can cook over charcoal. It takes three or four minutes, it soaks up smoke in that short window, and its natural sweetness turns almost candy-like where the edges char. Pile it into warm tortillas with a sharp slaw and a cool lime crema and you have a dinner that tastes like a beach vacation but comes together faster than ordering takeout.

This guide covers buying shrimp, a quick marinade, skewering tricks so nothing falls through the grate, and every component you need for a complete taco spread.

Buying the right shrimp

For grilling, size matters. Small shrimp overcook in seconds and slip through the grate. Aim for large or jumbo shrimp, labeled 16/20 or 21/25, meaning that many shrimp per pound. Plan on a third of a pound per person for tacos.

Almost all shrimp sold "fresh" at the seafood counter was previously frozen and thawed. You are often better off buying bags of frozen shrimp and thawing them yourself so they are as fresh as possible when you cook. Look for shrimp frozen without added sodium tripolyphosphate, which makes them retain water and steam instead of sear. The ingredient list should say just shrimp, or shrimp and salt.

Peeled and deveined with tails on is the most convenient. Pull the tails off before stuffing tacos, or leave them on and let people do it themselves.

To thaw: put the frozen shrimp in a colander under cold running water for 5 to 8 minutes, tossing occasionally. Then dry thoroughly on paper towels. Wet shrimp will not char.

The marinade

For 1.5 pounds of shrimp (feeds four):

  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil or olive oil
  • Zest of 1 lime
  • 2 cloves garlic, grated
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon brown sugar

Toss the shrimp with the marinade and let it sit at room temperature for 15 minutes while the coals light, or refrigerated up to an hour. Do not add lime juice yet; acid begins "cooking" shrimp, turning them rubbery if they sit too long. Save the juice for the end.

Skewering for success

Shrimp curl and spin on a single skewer, which makes flipping frustrating. Use two parallel skewers about half an inch apart, threading each shrimp through both the head end and the tail end. The shrimp lie flat and flip as one unit.

If you use bamboo skewers, soak them for 30 minutes first. Metal skewers are better; they conduct heat into the shrimp and never burn.

Pack the shrimp close together but not squished. Four to six shrimp per double skewer is a good size for easy handling.

The fire

Shrimp want direct, high heat. Fire up a full chimney, then pour and rake the coals evenly across about two-thirds of the grill, leaving one end empty as a safety zone. Heat the grate for five minutes, brush it clean, and oil it well.

On the Slot Grill Compact, four double skewers fit side by side over the coals, which handles dinner for two to three. On the Full Size you can do the whole batch at once with room left for tortillas and peppers.

Cooking the shrimp

Lay the skewers over the hottest part of the fire. Cook without moving for about 2 minutes, until the bottoms are opaque and lightly charred. Flip and cook another 1 to 2 minutes. Shrimp are done when they turn opaque throughout and bend into a relaxed C. Once they coil into a tight O, they have gone too far.

If you want a number, shrimp are safe at 145°F, which arrives very quickly. The color change is a reliable signal.

Pull the skewers onto a platter and immediately squeeze half a lime over them. Slide the shrimp off and chop them roughly, or leave them whole.

Lime crema

Whisk together:

  • 1/2 cup sour cream or Mexican crema
  • 2 tablespoons mayonnaise
  • Zest and juice of 1 lime
  • 1 small clove garlic, grated
  • Pinch of salt

Thin with a teaspoon of water if it is too thick to drizzle. Add a spoonful of adobo sauce from a can of chipotles for a smoky variation.

Crunchy cabbage slaw

The slaw is what makes these tacos addictive, so do not skip it.

  • 3 cups finely shredded green or red cabbage (or a mix)
  • 1 small red onion, very thinly sliced
  • 1 jalapeño, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
  • Juice of 2 limes
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar

Toss everything together 15 to 30 minutes before eating. The salt and lime soften the cabbage slightly while keeping its crunch.

Warming tortillas

Corn tortillas are traditional and add great flavor; flour tortillas are softer and more forgiving. Either way, warm them right on the grate for 15 to 30 seconds per side, until they puff and pick up a few spots of char. Stack them in a clean kitchen towel to keep them warm and pliable.

For corn tortillas, double them up for each taco so they do not tear.

Assembling

Build each taco in this order: a smear of crema, a pile of shrimp, a handful of slaw, then toppings. Good toppings include:

  • Sliced avocado or quick guacamole
  • Crumbled cotija or feta
  • Pickled red onions
  • Grilled pineapple, diced
  • Fresh salsa or a green tomatillo sauce
  • Extra lime wedges and hot sauce

Grilled pineapple salsa

Since the fire is already hot, grill a few rings of fresh pineapple for 2 to 3 minutes per side until caramelized. Dice and toss with diced red onion, minced jalapeño, cilantro, lime juice and salt. The sweet, smoky salsa is a perfect partner for the spice on the shrimp.

Making it a griddle meal

If you have a griddle plate, you can cook the shrimp unskewered right on the flat steel. Get the griddle very hot over direct coals, add a slick of oil, and spread the shrimp in a single layer. Cook 90 seconds, flip with a spatula, and cook another minute. You lose a little smoke flavor but gain a more even sear, and the griddle is a great place to char peppers and onions alongside.

Swaps and variations

Fish tacos: use firm white fish such as mahi-mahi, snapper or halibut. Marinate the same way, grill on a well-oiled grate or a griddle, and flake into the tortillas.

Chicken: boneless thighs in the same marinade, grilled over direct heat until 175°F, then chopped.

Vegetarian: thick slices of poblano, red onion, zucchini and corn kernels cut off grilled cobs, with black beans warmed in a pan on the grate.

Timeline for a stress-free taco night

  1. 45 minutes before: thaw and dry shrimp, mix the marinade and toss.
  2. 35 minutes: light the charcoal and soak skewers if using bamboo.
  3. 30 minutes: make the slaw and crema.
  4. 15 minutes: thread skewers, set up toppings.
  5. 10 minutes: grill pineapple and make the salsa.
  6. 5 minutes: grill shrimp, then warm tortillas while the shrimp rest.

Common mistakes

Overcooking. This is the number-one problem. Shrimp go from perfect to rubbery in less than a minute. Pull them as soon as they turn opaque.

A crowded skewer. Shrimp packed too tightly steam instead of char. Leave a sliver of space.

A cold grill. Without high heat you get gray shrimp and no flavor. Wait for the coals to fully light.

Wet shrimp. Dry them well before marinating.

The takeaway

Shrimp tacos are the fastest way to get real charcoal flavor on a weeknight. Light the coals, prep the slaw while they heat, and dinner is done in minutes. Once you have the double-skewer trick down, you will make these all summer long.

Shrimp tacos for a party

Taco bars are one of the easiest ways to feed a crowd, because guests build their own plates and you only need to keep the shrimp coming. For 10 to 12 people, figure on 4 pounds of shrimp, three dozen tortillas, a double batch of slaw and crema, and at least three salsas. Grill shrimp in waves of six to eight skewers so each batch goes out hot, and keep a stack of warm tortillas wrapped in a towel inside a small cooler, which acts like an insulated bread box for up to an hour.

Set the toppings in a line in the order people build: tortillas, crema, protein, slaw, then the fun stuff. Put the hot sauce and lime wedges at the very end so they do not clog the line. If you want to stretch the protein, add a pan of black beans simmered with cumin, garlic and a splash of beer right on the cool side of the grill.

What to drink

Something cold and citrusy. A pitcher of agua fresca made from blended watermelon, lime and a little sugar is great for all ages. For adults, a light lager with a lime wedge or a simple margarita on the rocks keeps the mood relaxed and pairs naturally with the chili and smoke.

Quick questions

What size shrimp is best for grilling?

Large or jumbo, labeled 16/20 or 21/25 per pound. Smaller shrimp overcook quickly and fall through the grate.

How do I keep shrimp from spinning on the skewer?

Thread each shrimp through two parallel skewers about half an inch apart.

How do I know when grilled shrimp are done?

The flesh goes fully opaque and bends into a relaxed C, usually 3 to 4 minutes total over high heat.
