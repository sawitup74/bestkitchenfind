Make-Ahead Cookout Sides That Steal the Show

Ten sides you can make a day ahead, from tangy potato salad to charred corn salad, plus how to keep them safe and fresh outdoors.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

The grill gets all the attention at a cookout, but sides are what fill the plate. A good spread of side dishes turns a pile of burgers into a feast. The trick is choosing sides that can be made ahead, travel well and hold up in warm weather, so you can focus on the grill when guests arrive. Here are ten reliable favorites, along with tips for keeping them fresh and safe.

What makes a great make-ahead side

  • It improves or holds overnight. Flavors meld, textures stay pleasant.
  • It tastes good cold or at room temperature.
  • It holds up in the heat. Vinaigrette-based salads are more forgiving than mayonnaise-heavy dishes, though both are fine with proper chilling.
  • It is easy to serve. One bowl, one spoon.

1. Tangy mustard potato salad

Boil 3 pounds of waxy potatoes, cut into chunks, in heavily salted water until just tender, about 12 minutes. Drain and, while still warm, toss with 2 tablespoons apple cider vinegar. The warm potatoes absorb the vinegar for deeper flavor.

When cool, fold in 3/4 cup mayonnaise, 2 tablespoons Dijon, 2 tablespoons yellow mustard, 3 chopped hard-boiled eggs, 2 chopped celery stalks, 1/2 diced red onion, 1/4 cup chopped dill pickles, fresh dill, salt, pepper and a dusting of paprika.

Make up to a day ahead. Keep chilled.

2. Charred corn salad

Grill 6 ears of corn until charred. Cut off the kernels and toss with halved cherry tomatoes, diced red onion, diced avocado (add just before serving), crumbled cotija or feta, chopped cilantro, lime juice, olive oil, chili powder and salt.

Make the base a day ahead; add avocado and herbs right before serving.

3. Vinegar slaw

Mayonnaise-free slaw holds well in the heat and cuts through rich barbecue. Shred 1 head of cabbage and 2 carrots. Whisk 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar, 1/4 cup sugar, 1/3 cup neutral oil, 1 teaspoon celery seed, 1 teaspoon salt and black pepper. Heat the dressing until the sugar dissolves and pour over the cabbage. Refrigerate overnight. It gets better as it sits.

4. Pasta salad with salami and provolone

Cook 1 pound of short pasta, such as rotini or fusilli, in well-salted water. Rinse with cold water and drain. Toss with diced salami, cubed provolone, halved cherry tomatoes, sliced pepperoncini, sliced black olives, chopped red onion, chopped parsley and a red wine vinaigrette with oregano and garlic.

Make a day ahead. Add a little extra vinaigrette before serving, since pasta absorbs dressing overnight.

5. Watermelon, cucumber and feta salad

Cube a small seedless watermelon and two cucumbers. Toss with thinly sliced red onion, crumbled feta, fresh mint and a dressing of lime juice, olive oil and salt. Make the components ahead and combine just before serving so the watermelon does not weep too much.

6. Classic baked beans

Cook diced bacon and onion in a Dutch oven. Add drained canned navy or pinto beans, ketchup, molasses, brown sugar, mustard, Worcestershire sauce and apple cider vinegar. Bake at 325°F for 1.5 to 2 hours, or simmer on the cool side of the grill. Make up to three days ahead; reheat on the grill.

7. Macaroni and cheese

Make a stovetop mac and cheese with sharp cheddar and a little smoked gouda. Transfer to a foil pan, top with buttered breadcrumbs and refrigerate. On cookout day, reheat covered on the cool side of the grill for 30 to 40 minutes, then uncover to crisp the top.

8. Grilled vegetable orzo

Cook 1 pound of orzo. Toss with chopped grilled zucchini, peppers and red onion, plus feta, basil, lemon zest, lemon juice and olive oil. Serve at room temperature. Make a day ahead and add fresh basil before serving.

9. Black bean and mango salad

Combine 2 cans drained black beans, 2 diced mangoes, 1 diced red bell pepper, diced red onion, chopped cilantro, a minced jalapeño, lime juice, olive oil, cumin and salt. Holds well for a day and travels easily.

10. Cornbread

Bake a skillet of cornbread the day before, wrap it tightly and store at room temperature. Reheat wrapped in foil on the cool side of the grill for 15 minutes before serving. Serve with honey butter.

Keeping sides safe outdoors

Warm weather and food that sits out are a risky combination. Bacteria multiply quickly between 40°F and 140°F, the "danger zone."

  • Keep cold sides cold. Refrigerate until right before serving. Set bowls in larger bowls filled with ice.
  • Serve smaller portions. Put out a portion and replenish from the fridge or cooler as needed.
  • Follow the two-hour rule. Discard perishable foods left out more than two hours, or one hour if it is above 90°F.
  • Keep hot sides hot. Hold them above 140°F on the cool side of the grill.
  • Shade matters. Keep the food table out of direct sun.

Mayonnaise myths

Many people believe mayonnaise is the main food safety culprit at picnics. Commercial mayonnaise is actually acidic and does not spoil easily. The bigger risk usually comes from the other ingredients, like potatoes, eggs and pasta, that have been mixed in. Treat any mixed salad as perishable and keep it cold.

Transporting sides

  • Use containers with tight-fitting lids.
  • Pack coolers with plenty of ice or ice packs.
  • Put cold sides on the bottom, near the ice.
  • Keep hot sides wrapped in foil and towels in a separate insulated cooler.

Portion guide

For a cookout with several sides, plan about 1/2 to 3/4 cup of each side per person. With four or five sides, that is plenty. For 20 people, make about 10 to 15 cups of each.

Planning your sides

Aim for balance:

  • One starchy side: potato salad, pasta salad, mac and cheese or cornbread.
  • One fresh, crunchy side: coleslaw, corn salad or a green salad.
  • One fruity side: watermelon salad or fruit platter.
  • One hot side: baked beans or grilled vegetables.

The takeaway

The best cookout sides are made the day before, travel easily and taste great cold or at room temperature. Plan a balanced mix, prep ahead, keep cold foods cold, and you will have more time to spend at the grill and with your guests.

Going further: holding, transporting and serving sides

Know which sides travel

Vinegar-dressed slaws, grain salads, bean salads and pickles hold well for hours in a cooler and often taste better the next day. Mayonnaise-based salads are fine too, as long as they stay cold, at 40°F or below. Leafy green salads wilt quickly; pack dressing separately and toss at the last minute.

Serve cold sides cold

Nest serving bowls in larger bowls of ice. Keep backup portions in the cooler and refill rather than setting everything out at once. Follow the 2-hour rule for perishables, or 1 hour when it is above 90°F.

Finish on the fire

Some make-ahead sides are even better with a quick turn on the grill:

  • Par-boiled potatoes, tossed in oil, grilled until crisp and finished with herbs.
  • Corn on the cob, husked at home and grilled until charred, then rolled in compound butter.
  • Halved romaine hearts, charred for a minute and dressed with a creamy dressing.
  • Thick slices of day-old bread, toasted over the coals and rubbed with garlic.

Season again before serving

Cold dulls flavor. Taste every make-ahead side right before serving and add a pinch of salt, a squeeze of lemon or a splash of vinegar. It makes a big difference.

Containers that make life easier

Use flat, lidded containers for salads, which chill fast and stack well. Label each with the dish and the date. Bring a few serving spoons in a zip bag so nobody is scrambling.

Quick questions

Which cookout sides can be made a day ahead?

Potato salad, vinegar slaw, pasta salad, baked beans, mac and cheese, corn salad and cornbread all make ahead well.

Is mayonnaise dangerous at picnics?

Commercial mayo is acidic and fairly stable. The bigger risk comes from mixed-in foods like potatoes and eggs, so keep salads cold.

How much of each side do I need per person?

About 1/2 to 3/4 cup of each side when serving four or five sides.
