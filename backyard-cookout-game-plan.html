The Backyard Cookout Game Plan

A week-out to last-bite timeline for hosting a relaxed, delicious backyard cookout, with menus, shopping math, setup and cleanup.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

A great backyard cookout looks effortless. The host is laughing with guests, food comes off the grill at a steady pace, drinks are cold and there is somewhere to sit in the shade. Behind that easy vibe is a plan. The difference between a stressful party and a fun one is almost always preparation: what you do in the days and hours before guests arrive.

This guide is a complete game plan, from a week out until the last dish is washed.

One week out: decide the shape of the party

Answer a few questions first:

  • How many people? Get an approximate head count, including kids.
  • What time? Lunch cookouts are casual; evening cookouts last longer and need lights.
  • What style? A sit-down dinner, a grazing party, or a drop-in afternoon?
  • Dietary needs? Ask about vegetarians, allergies and gluten-free guests.
  • What is the weather plan? Have a backup for rain: a tent, porch, garage or alternate date.

Choose a menu that cooks itself

The best cookout menus include a mix of quick-cooking and make-ahead items, so you are not chained to the grill. A good formula:

  • Two main proteins: one quick (burgers, sausages, chicken thighs, skewers) and one showpiece (a spatchcocked chicken, a big steak, a pork tenderloin).
  • One vegetarian main: grilled vegetable platter, halloumi skewers, or portobello burgers.
  • Three sides: at least two that can be made a day ahead.
  • One dessert: something you can make ahead or grill quickly.
  • Snacks: for guests to graze on before the main event.

Sample menu for 12

  • Smash burgers with all the toppings
  • Lemon-herb chicken thighs
  • Grilled vegetable platter with herby dressing (doubles as the vegetarian main)
  • Creamy potato salad
  • Watermelon, cucumber and feta salad
  • Corn on the cob with chili-lime butter
  • Chips, salsa and guacamole
  • Grilled peaches with vanilla ice cream
  • Lemonade, iced tea and a cooler of drinks

Shopping math

How much food do you need? Here is a reliable guide per adult:

ItemPer person
Burgers1.5 patties
Hot dogs or sausages1.5 links
Chicken (bone-in)2 pieces
Steak or boneless meat6–8 oz
Side dishes (total)1–1.5 cups
Corn1 ear
Buns1 more than proteins
Drinks2 per hour for the first 2 hours, 1 per hour after
Ice1–2 lbs

Kids eat roughly half. Always buy a little extra; leftover burgers and sausages freeze well.

Three days out: shop and prep

  • Shop for non-perishables, charcoal, paper goods and drinks.
  • Check your grill and tools. Make sure you have enough charcoal for several hours.
  • Make sauces, rubs, dressings and marinades.
  • Clean the patio and outdoor furniture.

The day before: do everything you can

  • Shop for fresh produce and meat.
  • Marinate the chicken.
  • Form burger patties or portion beef balls for smash burgers and refrigerate between layers of parchment.
  • Make potato salad, pasta salad, slaws (dress them later) and desserts.
  • Chop toppings: onions, tomatoes, lettuce, pickles.
  • Freeze extra ice and chill drinks.
  • Set up tables, chairs and shade.

Morning of: the final prep

  • Grill the vegetable platter in the morning and let it marinate in its dressing.
  • Set out serving platters, utensils and labels.
  • Fill coolers with ice and drinks.
  • Arrange a condiment station.
  • Prep a grill kit: tongs, spatulas, thermometer, gloves, paper towels, oil and sheet pans.

Setting up the space

Zones

Organize your yard into zones:

  • Grill zone: away from foot traffic, kids and pets. A clear landing area for food going on and off.
  • Food table: in the shade, near the grill but not right next to it.
  • Drink station: away from the food table to prevent bottlenecks.
  • Seating: in the shade.
  • Kid zone: lawn games, bubbles, a sprinkler.

Grill setup

Set the grill up on a stable, non-flammable surface. Thick steel grills with an air gap underneath, like the Slot Grill, can sit on tables and hard surfaces, but keep them away from flammable materials, siding, overhanging branches and children.

For a crowd, the Full Size is the right tool: its 231 square inches of cooking area fits a dozen burgers or several pounds of chicken at once. Some hosts run two grills: a Full Size for main proteins and a Compact for sides, corn and buns.

Two hours before: light the fire

  1. Light the charcoal about 45 minutes before you plan to start cooking.
  2. Bank the coals on one side for a two-zone setup.
  3. Grill the corn first, since it holds well.
  4. Cook the chicken thighs next. They take about 40 minutes and can rest on a sheet pan.

Guests arrive: keep it moving

  • Put out snacks and drinks immediately.
  • Start grilling quick items as people settle in.
  • Serve food in waves rather than all at once. Burgers come off hot and fresh, and nobody waits too long.
  • Refill ice and drinks.

Food safety outdoors

  • Keep cold food cold: at or below 40°F. Use ice baths under salads and dips.
  • Keep hot food hot: at or above 140°F. Hold cooked food on the cool side of the grill or in an insulated container.
  • Two-hour rule: discard perishable food left out for more than two hours, or one hour if it is above 90°F.
  • Separate raw and cooked: use separate plates, tongs and cutting boards.
  • Check temperatures: burgers 160°F, chicken 165°F, pork chops 145°F.

Dessert on the grill

As the coals die down, they are perfect for dessert:

  • Grilled peaches: halve, pit, brush with butter and grill cut-side down for 3 to 4 minutes. Serve with ice cream.
  • Grilled pineapple: with a brown sugar and cinnamon glaze.
  • S'mores: a crowd favorite with kids and adults.
  • Banana boats: split bananas in the peel, pack the slit with chocolate chips and mini marshmallows, foil-wrap them and set them over the coals.

Cleanup

  • Set out clearly labeled trash and recycling bins.
  • Transfer leftovers to the refrigerator within two hours.
  • While the grill is still warm, scrape and oil the grates.
  • Let the coals burn out completely before disposing of the ash in a metal container.

The takeaway

Plan the menu, do the work ahead, set up zones, and cook in waves. With a good game plan, you will spend less time stressing and more time enjoying your own party.

Going further: hosting details people remember

Shade, seats and light

Comfort matters as much as food. Set up chairs in shade, provide a spot where guests can sit near the grill and chat, and string lights for evening. Have bug spray and sunscreen out.

A welcome bite

Have something ready the moment guests arrive: chips and salsa, a cheese board, or grilled sausage coins on toothpicks. Hungry guests waiting for the main meal crowd the grill.

Drinks station

Set the drinks away from the grill so traffic flows. Offer water, a non-alcoholic option, and one signature drink. Keep ice in a separate cooler from drinks.

The two-zone rule

A two-zone fire is the host's best friend. Sear burgers and sausages over the coals, then move them to the cooler side to hold while you finish the rest. This prevents overcooked food and keeps things flexible when guests arrive late.

Kids at the cookout

Mark the grill zone as adults only. Provide lawn games, chalk and a bubble station. Grill kid-friendly food first so they eat early.

Weather backup

Check the forecast. Have a pop-up canopy for sun or light rain, placed well away from the grill; never grill beneath it.

Cleanup plan

Set out clearly labeled trash and recycling bins. Ask a friend to help clear plates. Let the grill cool before cleaning; a scraper and a thin oil coat are enough for the night.

Cook's notebook

After the party

The next morning, scrape and oil the grates if you did not get to it the night before. Pack leftovers into shallow containers so they chill fast, and use cooked meats within 3 to 4 days. Leftover grilled chicken becomes tacos, leftover steak becomes a steak salad or breakfast hash on the griddle, and grilled vegetables become a pasta sauce. Write a quick note about what ran out and what was left over, so next time you know exactly how much to buy.

Quick questions

How much meat do I need per person for a cookout?

About 6 to 8 ounces of boneless meat per adult, or 1.5 burgers or sausages. Kids eat about half.

When should I light the grill before a party?

About 45 minutes before you want to start cooking, so the coals are ready.

How long can food sit out at a cookout?

No more than two hours, or one hour if the temperature is above 90°F.
