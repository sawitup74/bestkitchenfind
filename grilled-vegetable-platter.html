The Big Grilled Vegetable Platter

How to grill every vegetable in the market, from peppers to radicchio, plus a herby dressing that turns them into a showpiece.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

A platter of charred, glistening vegetables might be the most impressive thing you can put on a cookout table. It is colorful, it feeds vegetarians and meat lovers alike, and it can be made ahead and served at room temperature. The problem is that every vegetable cooks differently. Zucchini is done in minutes; a whole onion needs half an hour. This guide breaks it down so everything ends up perfectly tender with a touch of char.

The basic method

Nearly every vegetable follows the same three steps:

  1. Cut for the grill. Make pieces big enough not to fall through the grate and uniform enough to cook evenly. Long planks, thick rounds and halves are your friends.
  2. Oil and season. Toss with just enough olive oil to coat, plus salt and pepper. Too much oil causes flare-ups and greasy results.
  3. Grill and dress. Cook over the appropriate heat until tender and marked, then dress while still warm so they soak up flavor.

Setting up the fire

A two-zone fire is essential for vegetables. Hardy items start over direct heat to char, then slide to the cool side to finish softening. Delicate items stay over medium heat the whole time.

On a Slot Grill Full Size, pour a full chimney of lit coals into about half the grill. The other side becomes your finishing zone and holding area. On the Compact, which is ideal for a smaller platter for two to four people, bank the coals at one end and cook in rounds.

Zucchini and summer squash

Cut lengthwise into thick planks rather than rounds. Planks lie flat, will not fall through, and develop beautiful long grill marks. Score the cut side lightly in a crosshatch pattern so they absorb the dressing. Pull them when just tender; overcooked zucchini turns to mush.

Eggplant

Eggplant must be fully cooked; underdone eggplant is spongy and bitter. Brush generously with oil, because it absorbs a lot, and cook until creamy throughout. Japanese and Italian varieties are excellent grilled.

Peppers

For silky, sweet peppers, grill them whole over high heat, turning until the skin is blackened all over. Drop them in a bowl, cover with a plate for 10 minutes, then peel off the charred skin. For a crunchier texture, cut into quarters and grill skin-side down until blistered.

Onions

Onion rounds separate and fall into the fire unless you skewer them horizontally with a toothpick or skewer before grilling. Start over direct heat for color, then move to the cool side until they are sweet and soft. Red onions look the most striking on a platter.

Corn

Husked corn grilled over direct heat develops wonderful charred kernels. Brush with butter or oil and turn every two minutes. For juicier corn, soak unhusked ears in water for 20 minutes and grill in the husk for 15 minutes, then shuck and char briefly.

Mushrooms

Portobello caps are meaty and satisfying. Brush with oil, balsamic and garlic and grill gill-side down first. Button or cremini mushrooms work best skewered.

Leafy vegetables

Halved heads of romaine, radicchio, endive or baby bok choy develop a smoky, slightly bitter edge when grilled briefly over high heat. Brush the cut side with oil, grill cut-side down for a minute, flip and grill another minute. The center should stay crisp.

Hard vegetables

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots and beets are too dense to cook through on the grill before they burn. Parboil or microwave them until just tender, then cut, oil and grill to finish with char.

The dressing

The secret to a great platter is dressing the vegetables while they are hot. Make this herby vinaigrette before you start grilling:

  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 3 tablespoons red wine or sherry vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 small shallot, minced
  • 1 clove garlic, grated
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1/2 cup mixed chopped soft herbs (parsley, basil, mint, chives, dill)
  • 1 teaspoon honey
  • Salt and pepper

Shake everything in a jar. As each batch of vegetables comes off the grill, lay them on a sheet pan and spoon some dressing over them. The vegetables will absorb it as they cool.

Building the platter

Arrange vegetables in clusters by type on a large board or platter, mixing colors. Tuck in grilled lemon halves for squeezing. Finish with:

  • Crumbled feta, goat cheese, or torn burrata
  • Toasted pine nuts, almonds or pistachios
  • Flaky salt and a drizzle of good olive oil
  • Fresh herbs and a few flowers if you have them

Serve with a bowl of garlicky yogurt, hummus or romesco, plus grilled bread brushed with olive oil.

Making it ahead

Grilled vegetables are one of the best make-ahead dishes. Grill up to a day in advance, toss with dressing, and refrigerate. Bring to room temperature an hour before serving and add the fresh herbs, cheese and nuts at the last minute. The flavors deepen overnight.

Turn it into a meal

  • Antipasto night: add cured meats, marinated olives, fresh mozzarella and crusty bread.
  • Grain bowls: toss chopped vegetables with farro, quinoa or rice, the vinaigrette, chickpeas and feta.
  • Sandwiches: layer eggplant, peppers, zucchini, pesto and fresh mozzarella on ciabatta, press and toast it on the grill.
  • Pasta: chop and toss with hot pasta, olive oil, garlic, parmesan and basil.

Common mistakes

Pieces too small. They fall through or overcook. Go big and slice after grilling if needed.

Too much oil. It drips, flares and leaves a sooty taste. Toss lightly to coat.

Under-seasoning. Vegetables need more salt than you think. Season before and after grilling.

Crowding. Vegetables packed tightly steam. Cook in batches and give each piece some room.

The takeaway

Think of the grill as a way to transform vegetables, not just cook them. A little fire, a little patience and a bright dressing turn ordinary produce into the dish everyone asks about. Make a big platter for your next cookout, and watch it disappear before the burgers.

A shopping list for a platter that feeds ten

Buy more than you think, because vegetables shrink as they cook and lose about a quarter of their volume. For ten people as a side dish: four zucchini or yellow squash, two large eggplants, four bell peppers in mixed colors, three red onions, six ears of corn, two bunches of asparagus, eight portobello caps or a pound of cremini, two pints of cherry tomatoes, two bunches of scallions and two heads of radicchio or romaine. Add a block of feta, a handful of nuts and two big bunches of mixed herbs.

Order of operations on the grill

Start with the slow items so they can rest on the cool side while you work: onions, fennel, peppers you plan to peel, and parcooked potatoes. Next go eggplant and corn. Zucchini, mushrooms and asparagus follow. The last two minutes belong to the leafy items, scallions and tomatoes, which barely need to touch the heat. Following this order means everything finishes within a few minutes of each other and nothing sits for an hour losing its snap.

Seasoning beyond salt

Once you are comfortable with the basic platter, try a different finishing profile each time. A sprinkle of za'atar and a drizzle of tahini turn the platter Middle Eastern. Smoked paprika, sherry vinegar and toasted almonds push it toward Spain. Chili crisp, rice vinegar and toasted sesame seeds give it a punchy, savory edge. The method never changes, only the last thirty seconds.

Quick questions

Do I need to oil vegetables before grilling?

Yes, a light coat keeps them from sticking and helps them brown. Too much oil causes flare-ups.

How do I keep onion rings from falling apart on the grill?

Skewer thick rounds horizontally with a toothpick or skewer before grilling.

Can grilled vegetables be made ahead?

Yes. Grill and dress up to a day ahead, refrigerate, and bring to room temperature before serving.
