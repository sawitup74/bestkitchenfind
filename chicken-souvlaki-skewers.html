Chicken Souvlaki Skewers with Homemade Tzatziki

Lemon-oregano marinated chicken, charred over coals and wrapped in warm pita: a Greek street food classic built for backyard grills.

7 min read · Best Kitchen Find test kitchen

Souvlaki is the Greek answer to the question: what is the best thing you can do with a little meat, a hot fire and a handful of pantry staples? Chunks of marinated chicken are threaded onto skewers, charred over charcoal, and wrapped in soft pita with garlicky yogurt, tomatoes and onions. It is fast, it feeds a crowd, and the leftover components make the best lunch of the week.

What makes great souvlaki

Three things define it: a bold lemon and oregano marinade, high-heat cooking that chars the edges while keeping the middle juicy, and generous accompaniments. Getting those right is easier than you might think, as long as you choose the right cut of chicken and do not overcrowd the skewers.

Choose thighs, not breasts

Boneless, skinless chicken thighs are the best choice for souvlaki. They stay moist over intense heat, they have more flavor than breast meat, and they forgive a minute or two of extra cooking. If you must use breasts, brine them first for 30 minutes in a solution of 1 tablespoon salt per 2 cups of water, and pull them at exactly 160°F, letting carryover heat take them to 165°F.

Plan on about 6 ounces of chicken per person. For four people, buy 1.5 to 2 pounds of boneless thighs.

The marinade

For 2 pounds of chicken:

  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • Juice of 1 large lemon plus the zest
  • 4 cloves garlic, grated or pressed
  • 1 tablespoon dried oregano, preferably Greek
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 2 tablespoons plain Greek yogurt
  • 1 tablespoon red wine vinegar
  • 2 teaspoons kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper

The yogurt is not strictly traditional, but it helps the marinade cling and tenderizes the chicken gently. Whisk everything together.

Prep the chicken

Trim off any large pieces of fat and cut each thigh into 1.5-inch chunks. Try to keep the pieces about the same size so they cook evenly.

Toss with the marinade in a bowl or zip-top bag. Marinate in the refrigerator for 2 to 8 hours. Even 30 minutes adds flavor if you are short on time.

Skewering

Thread the chicken onto metal skewers or soaked bamboo skewers, folding thinner pieces in half so each chunk is roughly a cube. Pack the pieces snugly but do not crush them together. Leave about an inch bare at each end.

Flat metal skewers are ideal because the chicken does not spin when you turn them.

Do not put vegetables on the same skewers as the meat. Onions and peppers cook at different rates and tend to burn or stay raw. Grill them separately.

The fire

Souvlaki is cooked over direct, high heat. Fire up a full chimney, then pour and rake the coals across most of the grill, leaving one end with fewer coals as a cooler zone for parking skewers that are browning too fast.

Grilling

  1. Lay the skewers over the hottest part of the fire.
  2. Grill for 2 to 3 minutes per side, turning four times total, until all sides are charred and the chicken reaches 175°F in the center of the largest piece. Total time is about 10 to 12 minutes.
  3. Baste with a little extra olive oil mixed with lemon juice and oregano as they cook, if you like.
  4. Move finished skewers to the cooler end or a platter, and squeeze fresh lemon over the top.

Homemade tzatziki

The cool, garlicky sauce is non-negotiable.

  • 1 English cucumber
  • 1.5 cups whole-milk Greek yogurt
  • 2 cloves garlic, finely grated
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice or red wine vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh dill or mint
  • Salt to taste

Grate the cucumber on the large holes of a box grater. Put it in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze hard to remove as much water as possible. This keeps the tzatziki thick. Stir everything together and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes to let the garlic mellow. It keeps for four days.

Warm the pita

Brush pita rounds lightly with olive oil and grill for 20 to 30 seconds per side, until warm, soft and lightly marked. Stack in a towel to keep warm.

Build the wrap

Lay a warm pita on a square of foil or parchment. Spread with tzatziki, then add:

  • Chunks of chicken pulled from the skewer
  • Sliced ripe tomato
  • Thinly sliced red onion tossed with a pinch of sumac or paprika
  • A few crisp fries tucked inside, if you want to go full Athens street food
  • Chopped parsley
  • A sprinkle of dried oregano

Roll it up tightly in the foil or parchment so it holds together.

Make it a platter

For a dinner party, serve souvlaki as a platter instead: pile the skewers over a bed of lemon rice or herbed orzo, with tzatziki, a big Greek salad, grilled vegetables, olives and warm pita on the side. People assemble their own plates.

Greek salad

Cut ripe tomatoes and cucumbers into large chunks. Add sliced red onion, green bell pepper and kalamata olives. Dress with good olive oil, red wine vinegar, salt and dried oregano. Top with a thick slab of feta. No lettuce in the authentic version.

Lemon potatoes

Cut potatoes into wedges, toss with olive oil, lemon juice, garlic, oregano and salt, and roast in a hot oven or in a foil pan on the cool side of the grill until golden and tender.

Other proteins

The same marinade and method work beautifully with:

  • Pork shoulder or tenderloin: pork souvlaki is actually the most common in Greece. Use shoulder for more fat and flavor, cut into 1-inch pieces. Cook to 145°F for tenderloin or 160°F for shoulder.
  • Lamb leg: cut into 1.5-inch cubes and cook to medium, about 135°F.
  • Halloumi and vegetables: cubes of halloumi cheese, zucchini, red onion and cherry tomatoes on separate skewers for a vegetarian version.
  • Swordfish: firm enough for skewers, cook just to 135°F.

Prepping ahead for a party

Everything except the grilling can be done the day before. Marinate the chicken overnight and skewer it in the morning; keep the skewers covered on a sheet pan in the refrigerator. Make the tzatziki and chop the toppings ahead. When guests arrive, all you need to do is light the fire and grill.

Troubleshooting

Chicken is dry. You used breasts without brining, or overcooked. Switch to thighs.

Chicken is burnt outside, raw inside. The pieces are too large or the fire too hot. Cut into consistent 1.5-inch chunks and move skewers to the cooler zone if they char too fast.

Bamboo skewers caught fire. Soak longer, or let the exposed ends hang off the side of the hot zone.

Tzatziki is watery. The cucumber was not squeezed. Strain the finished tzatziki in a sieve for 20 minutes if needed.

The takeaway

Souvlaki rewards simplicity. A good marinade, a hot fire and a little care with skewering are all you need for a dinner that feels like a summer night in a Greek taverna. Make extra; the leftovers disappear fast.

Cook's notebook

Building better skewers

Cut the chicken into even 1 1/4-inch pieces so everything cooks at the same rate. Thread them snugly but not crammed together; a small gap lets heat reach every side. If you use wooden skewers, soak them in water for 30 minutes first so they do not burn. Flat metal skewers stop the chunks from twirling every time you rotate them. Cook the vegetables on separate skewers from the meat, because onions and peppers cook at a different pace than chicken. Grill over direct heat, turning every couple of minutes, and check with a thermometer for 165°F in the thickest piece. Serve with warm pita charred briefly on the grates, a bowl of tzatziki and a squeeze of lemon over everything at the end.

Quick questions

Is chicken thigh or breast better for souvlaki?

Thigh. It stays juicy over high heat and has more flavor. Brine breasts if you use them.

How long should souvlaki marinate?

Two to eight hours in the refrigerator is ideal; 30 minutes still adds flavor.

Can I grill vegetables on the same skewer as chicken?

It is better not to. Vegetables cook at different rates, so grill them on their own skewers.
